# Local Growth Strategy: FISH Window Cleaning — Chicago Western & Southern Suburbs

_Research date: September 20, 2026_

## Executive decision

This should be built as a **local revenue engine**, not as a collection of SEO pages.

The North Star is:

> **Incremental contribution profit from completed jobs attributable to marketing.**

The operating metric beneath it is:

> **Cost per qualified estimate that becomes a completed, paid job**, segmented by service, town, and channel.

Traffic, impressions, rankings, form fills, and raw calls are diagnostic metrics. They are not the goal. A cheap lead that is outside the territory, unreachable, price-shopping, or never books is not a win.

The fastest acquisition sequence is:

1. Protect and improve the existing Google Business Profile and reviews.
2. Launch or optimize Google Local Services Ads using the existing real business identity and Business Profile.
3. Audit the current Google Ads account before creating overlapping campaigns; then run tightly controlled high-intent Search campaigns with call and lead-form assets.
4. Build an approved, conversion-focused site on one domain, with a small number of genuinely useful service and city pages.
5. Add Meta only as a measured creative test and retargeting layer—not as the first or primary source of urgent demand.
6. Build SEO, reviews, local links, and neighborhood proof as the compounding layer.

## The non-negotiable launch gate: brand and franchise authorization

Before publishing `fishchicagoland.com`, using the FISH name/logo on a new site, copying corporate site content, or pointing the Business Profile at the new domain, obtain written approval under the current franchise agreement and brand standards.

Why this matters:

- FISH's current public Terms of Use say its site content is protected and cannot be reproduced or republished without express permission, and that the FISH marks cannot be displayed or used without prior written permission.
- A FISH-branded domain can create trademark and franchise-contract risk even when operated by a legitimate franchisee; the franchise agreement controls the scope of the license.
- ICANN's domain-dispute policy expressly covers domains that are confusingly similar to a mark and used to attract users through confusion about affiliation.
- An old public FISH franchise agreement is not reliable enough to govern the current relationship, but it demonstrates why the executed current agreement—not an assumption—is the authority.

This is not a reason to stop marketing. The low-risk immediate route is to send ads to the existing approved FISH location page, use Google-hosted lead/call assets, and use the existing Business Profile while approval is obtained.

Written approval should cover:

- domain ownership and what happens to it if the franchise ends;
- use of the FISH name, logo, colors, photos, testimonials, and corporate copy;
- required disclosures and brand templates;
- whether the new site may collect leads or must send them to the corporate estimate form;
- ownership, privacy, storage, and reporting of lead data;
- use of Google Ads, Local Services Ads, Meta Ads, tracking numbers, and pixels;
- territory boundaries and prohibited towns/keywords;
- required review/approval workflow for creative and promotions.

Sources: [FISH Terms of Use](https://www.fishwindowcleaning.com/Chicagoland-IL-3289/terms-of-use), [ICANN UDRP](https://www.icann.org/en/contracted-parties/consensus-policies/uniform-domain-name-dispute-resolution-policy/uniform-domain-name-dispute-resolution-policy-25-02-2012-en), [FTC guide to buying a franchise](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/plain-language/pdf-0127_buying-a-franchise.pdf).

## What the existing digital footprint tells us

The current corporate location page has valuable trust signals but is weak as a focused acquisition page:

- It identifies Dave Weishar, the Bridgeview office, phone number `(630) 757-3474`, and 12 named service areas.
- It mixes residential, commercial, community, corporate, testimonial, recruiting, payment, and estimate content on one long page.
- It offers the services customers search for: residential and commercial window cleaning, gutter cleaning, pressure washing, awning cleaning, fixtures, fans, screens, skylights, and related work.
- It has a full estimate form, but without access to its analytics/tagging it may be difficult to connect an ad click to a qualified lead and ultimately to revenue.

The local organic competition already uses detailed town pages, local office/proximity proof, review counts, response-time promises, price guidance, before/after photos, and town-specific housing or commercial context. That means a generic page with the city name swapped will not be competitive or defensible.

Current page: [FISH Chicago Western & Southern Suburbs](https://www.fishwindowcleaning.com/Chicago-Western-Southern-Suburbs-3277/). ## Channel strategy

### 1. Google Business Profile: protect the local asset

Use the existing profile. Do **not** create one profile per town, use virtual offices, or create a second profile merely for the new website. Google permits one profile for a service-area business unless there are genuinely separate staffed locations with separate teams.

Actions:

- Confirm the real-world business name, primary category, secondary categories, phone, hours, service list, and service areas.
- Keep only towns/ZIPs actually served and inside the franchise territory. Google allows up to 20 service areas.
- If customers are not served at the Bridgeview office, configure the profile as a service-area business and hide the address as required by policy.
- Add real, current team-at-work images and before/after images every month. Avoid stock and materially AI-altered “proof” photos.
- Build a systematic, non-incentivized review request into job completion: direct link/QR, ask every customer, and respond to every review.
- Encourage honest specificity naturally (“What service did we perform and where?”) without scripting or gating reviews.
- Use posts for seasonal reminders and real project proof, but treat them as conversion support, not a magic ranking lever.
- Add the new website or booking link only after franchise approval and ensure it represents this exact location.

Google says local visibility is mainly determined by relevance, distance, and prominence; complete information, reviews, links, and photos support those factors. Sources: [local ranking factors](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091), [service-area rules](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481), [representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177), [review rules](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122), [photo guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6123536).

### 2. Local Services Ads: fastest high-intent test

Window cleaning is currently listed as a U.S. Local Services Ads category. LSA is Google-profile-led: select service areas and job types, pass verification, set budget/bidding, answer leads quickly, and build review quality. It does not require the proposed landing-page system to start.

Actions:

- Determine whether this franchise already has an LSA account and who owns/administers it.
- Connect the existing Business Profile so the business identity and reviews remain consistent.
- Complete business, insurance, license, and any required owner/worker verification.
- Select only profitable job types and territory-approved ZIPs/towns.
- Start with `Maximize leads` if there is enough budget to create learning volume; Google recommends a weekly budget capable of roughly 10 leads for its automated approach.
- Answer during advertised hours, respond rapidly, mark lead outcomes accurately, and dispute invalid leads where the product allows.
- Track every LSA lead through booked estimate, quoted value, won/lost, completed revenue, gross margin, and reason lost.

Sources: [U.S. LSA categories](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841/getting-started-with-local-services-ads-united-states), [screening and verification](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6226575), [service areas and job types](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7419052), [LSA bidding](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/10125017).

### 3. Google Search Ads: capture explicit demand

Search should be the first conventional paid-media channel because it captures people actively asking for the service. But first obtain read-only/admin access and audit the agency-run account. Do not blindly launch a second account bidding on the same towns and terms: it fragments data, can bid against the same business, and may create “unfair advantage” policy risk if used to occupy the same ad position.

Initial campaign design:

- Separate **residential window cleaning**, **commercial/storefront window cleaning**, and **gutter cleaning** because intent, economics, sales process, and seasonality differ.
- Start with exact and phrase match around high-intent terms: service + town, service + “near me,” quote/estimate, company/cleaner. Expand only from proven search-term data.
- Add negatives aggressively: jobs/careers, salary, DIY, supplies, equipment, training, high-rise if not offered, auto windows, free, and out-of-territory place names.
- Target `Presence: people in or regularly in` the approved locations, not Google's broader default that also includes people merely interested in the area.
- Run during hours when calls can be answered, unless after-hours forms/text follow-up are proven fast.
- Use location assets linked to the legitimate Business Profile, call assets with call reporting, sitelinks by service, images, and lead-form assets where appropriate.
- Send each ad to the closest matching page. Until an approved site exists, use the official location page plus Google-hosted call/lead assets.
- Do not start with broad Performance Max. It reduces query/channel control before clean conversion data exists. Revisit only after qualified-lead and booked-job feedback is flowing reliably.

Google explicitly recommends location-specific landing pages and provides a presence-only setting to reduce out-of-area clicks. Sources: [location landing pages](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404184), [presence-only targeting](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9376662), [ad assets](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7331111), [unfair advantage policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15936768).

### 4. Meta/Facebook/Instagram: second-wave demand generation

Meta can work for visual, seasonal household services, but the user is scrolling rather than searching. It should not replace Search/LSA at launch.

Use it for:

- retargeting visitors and engaged video viewers after enough audience accumulates;
- prior-customer seasonal reactivation where permission and platform terms allow;
- small, geographically tight prospecting tests with real before/after video;
- offers tied to a useful reason to act now: fall gutter/window bundle, holiday-ready windows, spring refresh, or exterior-only convenience.

Creative should show a real local crew, real results, the owner/local operation, insurance/trust proof, and one action. Test instant lead form versus landing page, but judge each on **booked-job cost**, not Meta's raw lead cost. Avoid purchased lists or customer uploads without appropriate rights and privacy compliance.

### 5. Local organic and partnerships: compounding, not instant

- Ask satisfied customers for reviews and permission to use photos/testimonials.
- Build relationships with realtors, property managers, HOAs, remodelers, painters, gutter/roof companies, and local business associations.
- Earn legitimate local mentions/links through community work and partnerships.
- Create useful seasonal content based on actual customer questions and local jobs, not generic AI articles.
- Commercial acquisition should also include outbound route-density campaigns: target clusters of storefronts and property managers where recurring contracts improve unit economics.

## Domain and site architecture

### Recommendation

Use **one approved domain with subdirectories**. Do not buy a domain per town and do not use a subdomain per town.

Google says it has no indexing/ranking preference between subdomains and subfolders. Subdirectories are simpler to manage, consolidate authority and analytics, reduce duplication, and make internal linking clearer. Multiple near-identical town domains/pages risk looking like doorway pages.

Preferred structure after brand approval:

```text
/
/residential-window-cleaning/
/commercial-window-cleaning/
/gutter-cleaning/
/service-areas/
/service-areas/hinsdale-il/
/service-areas/burr-ridge-il/
/service-areas/oak-brook-il/
/service-areas/downers-grove-il/
/about-dave-and-the-team/
/reviews/
/request-an-estimate/
```

Do not launch every service × every town combination. Start with core service pages and 3–5 priority town pages. Add a combined page such as `/service-areas/hinsdale-il/gutter-cleaning/` only when search demand, ad data, and genuinely distinct content justify it.

Sources: [Google on subfolders vs. subdomains](https://developers.google.com/search/help/crawling-index-faq), [spam/doorway policy](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies), [SEO starter guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide), [URL structure guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/url-structure).

### What makes a town page worth publishing

Every indexed town page needs evidence of actual usefulness:

- a town-specific value proposition and service availability;
- real jobs completed there, with customer permission where identifiable;
- original local photos and before/after proof;
- neighborhood/property context that actually affects the work;
- travel/dispatch expectations and truthful response-time promise;
- locally relevant testimonials with permission;
- service-specific FAQs based on real calls;
- clear phone and estimate action;
- links to related service pages and nearby served towns;
- consistent name, phone, service area, and business identity.

Do not publish invented local facts, fake testimonials, fake offices, unapproved customer logos, or paragraphs where only the city token changes. A local landmark mention is not “local proof.”

### Paid landing pages versus SEO pages

One page can serve both purposes when it is fast, useful, index-worthy, and matches the ad. For narrow experiments or nearly duplicative ad variants, use a canonical strategy or `noindex` rather than flooding the index.

A strong landing page should include:

1. Service + place + immediate outcome in the first screen.
2. Tap-to-call and short estimate form.
3. Real review rating/count and approved trust marks.
4. Real crew/project imagery.
5. What is included and what is not.
6. Residential or commercial path—not a muddled pitch.
7. Service area/availability and response expectation.
8. Process, insurance/safety, satisfaction pledge.
9. Local proof and FAQs.
10. Repeated single-purpose call to action.

## Measurement system

### Funnel events

Record these as distinct stages:

```text
Ad interaction
→ raw lead
→ reachable lead
→ qualified lead
→ estimate scheduled
→ estimate delivered
→ job won
→ job completed
→ revenue collected
→ contribution profit
→ repeat/referral revenue
```

### Required fields

Every lead record should contain:

- lead ID, timestamp, channel, campaign, ad group/ad, keyword/search term where available;
- source URL, landing page, GCLID/GBRAID/WBRAID or platform identifiers where lawfully collected;
- town/ZIP, requested service, residential/commercial, new/existing customer;
- call recording/outcome where permitted, contact status, qualification result;
- estimate amount, win/loss, loss reason, booked/completed date;
- revenue, direct job cost, gross/contribution margin;
- duplicate/spam/out-of-area flag.

### Scorecard

Report weekly by channel, service, and town:

- spend;
- qualified leads;
- cost per qualified lead;
- estimate rate;
- close rate;
- cost per booked and completed job;
- average completed-job revenue;
- contribution margin after ad spend;
- speed to first response;
- cancellation/no-show rate;
- new reviews and rating.

The bidding system should eventually optimize to **qualified or converted leads**, not every form submission. Google specifically recommends qualified-lead or converted-lead goals for enhanced conversions/offline imports. Sources: [offline conversion guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10029210), [call conversion imports](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6301373), [phone-call conversion tracking](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6100664).

### Economic guardrails

Calculate before scaling:

```text
Expected contribution profit per lead
= average completed-job contribution profit × lead-to-completed-job rate

Maximum rational CPL
= expected contribution profit per lead × desired acquisition-spend share
```

Example only: if a completed job contributes $220 before marketing, 30% of qualified leads become completed jobs, and the business will spend at most 40% of first-job contribution on acquisition, maximum qualified CPL is `$220 × 0.30 × 0.40 = $26.40`. Replace every assumption with actual business data. Include expected repeat value only after it is measured, not hoped for.

## First 30 days

### Days 1–3: access, safety, baseline

- Obtain the current franchise agreement/brand guide and request written digital-marketing approval.
- Inventory ownership/admin access for domain, GBP, Google Ads, LSA, GA4, Tag Manager, Search Console, Meta Business Manager, phone tracking, CRM, and corporate lead form.
- Export the last 12 months from the agency: spend, campaigns, locations, search terms, ads, landing pages, calls, forms, conversions, and change history.
- Export 12 months of jobs by town/service/source with revenue and status.
- Audit the existing GBP, review velocity, service areas, categories, photos, and duplicate listings.
- Define a qualified lead and the exact completed-job event.

### Days 4–7: immediate demand capture

- Start/repair LSA onboarding and verification for window cleaning.
- Coordinate with the current agency before changing or duplicating Search campaigns.
- Add/verify call reporting, lead-form assets, and location assets on approved Search activity.
- Enforce territory targeting and negatives.
- Implement a lead log and a same-business-day outcome workflow even if the first version is a spreadsheet.
- Start the review request process for all completed jobs.

### Days 8–14: approved landing system

- Finalize domain/branding authorization.
- Build the site shell, analytics, consent/privacy basics, call tracking, estimate routing, thank-you events, and offline outcome import.
- Publish the homepage, residential, commercial, gutter, service-area hub, and first priority town page.
- Use the official corporate form as the destination if a new form is not authorized.
- Verify mobile speed, tap-to-call, form delivery, spam protection, analytics, and accessibility.

### Days 15–30: experiments and expansion

- Launch matched Search landing pages one service/town cluster at a time.
- Use actual job data to choose the next 2–4 town pages.
- Run one Meta creative experiment only after lead handling and attribution work.
- Begin local partner outreach and collect original job photos/proof.
- Hold a weekly revenue review; pause waste quickly and expand only from qualified/job data.

## Initial priority model for towns and services

Do not prioritize merely by prestige or population. Score each combination on:

| Factor | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Historical completed-job contribution profit | 25% |
| Search demand and current impression share | 20% |
| Lead-to-job close rate | 15% |
| Route density / drive efficiency | 15% |
| Average ticket and repeat potential | 10% |
| Competitive intensity / expected CPC | 10% |
| Available local proof (photos, reviews, jobs) | 5% |

Until data is supplied, start research and creative preparation with **Hinsdale, Burr Ridge, Oak Brook, Downers Grove, and Clarendon Hills**, but activate only towns confirmed inside the contractual territory. Split residential and commercial. In September/fall, test gutter cleaning and window + gutter bundles without letting seasonal add-ons dilute core window-cleaning data.

## Decisions to make from evidence, not instinct

1. Does the franchise approve `fishchicagoland.com` and the exact use of FISH marks/content?
2. Who owns and controls the existing GBP, Google Ads, LSA, analytics, and customer data?
3. What did the current agency actually produce: spend → qualified leads → completed revenue?
4. What is the gross/contribution profit and close rate by residential/commercial service?
5. Which towns generate profitable route density rather than isolated jobs?
6. Is the Bridgeview office customer-facing or should the GBP address be hidden?
7. Can the local operation accept and respond to new leads quickly enough to justify more spend?

## Bottom line

The new website is valuable because it gives control over message match, conversion rate, tracking, and future SEO. It is **not** the fastest first source of leads, and hundreds of generated city/service pages would be a strategic mistake.

The winning sequence is: preserve the real Business Profile and reviews, activate LSA, take control of Search measurement, secure brand permission, publish a small high-quality local site, feed completed-job economics back into bidding, and expand pages/channels only where the numbers prove demand and operational fit.
