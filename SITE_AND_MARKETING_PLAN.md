## 1. Executive decision

We are not building a miniature version of the corporate FISH site and we are not building a giant city-page factory.

We are building a **Hinsdale-first local proof and conversion site** that complements Dave's official FISH location page. It will be optimized for booked, profitable work in a tight service cluster—not page count, clicks, impressions, or generic lead volume.

The launch wedge is:

- **Primary market:** Hinsdale, IL 60521
- **Supporting cluster:** Oak Brook, Burr Ridge, Western Springs, and Countryside
- **Primary offer:** residential and commercial window cleaning
- **Cross-sell services:** gutter cleaning and pressure washing, after verifying that each may be advertised under the franchise agreement
- **Proof:** more than a dozen commercial clients in Hinsdale; dozens of homes in Hinsdale and nearby towns; experience with historic homes and condominium buildings
- **Trust anchor:** Dave's official FISH franchise page, local Google reviews, and real local job photographs

Dave's existing FISH page remains the corporate identity and entity anchor. The owned site becomes the faster, more specific layer for campaigns, local proof, experiments, and closed-loop measurement.

### The north star

> **Incremental gross profit from attributable customers in the target cluster, after advertising and fulfillment costs.**

This is the metric Dave was asking for in his emails. Impressions, clicks, cost per lead, and form fills are diagnostic metrics. They are not the business outcome.

The operating scorecard must answer:

1. Which ZIP, service, and campaign created the opportunity?
2. Was it residential, retail storefront, or larger commercial work?
3. Did it become an estimate, a booked job, and collected revenue?
4. What was the gross profit after labor and media cost?
5. Did it create repeat work, a review, a referral, or nearby jobs?

## 4. Site architecture

### Domain decision

Use **one operator-controlled domain with folders**, subject to FISH approval.

Do not use:

- one domain per town;
- town subdomains;
- multiple thin microsites;
- an exact-match domain that implies a separate company or unauthorized ownership of the FISH mark;
- a Bridgeview-only domain when the commercial focus is Hinsdale and the surrounding cluster.

Why one domain:

- every useful local project strengthens the same site;
- authority, links, reviews, and engagement are not fragmented;
- tracking, maintenance, and advertising are simpler;
- new towns can be added only when evidence justifies them;
- it avoids looking like a lead-generation network or doorway-page scheme.

### Division of labor

| Asset | Job |
|---|---|
| Official FISH location page | Corporate identity, brand legitimacy, official location information, franchise relationship |
| Owned local site | Local proof, campaign landing pages, conversion testing, project stories, first-party measurement |
| Google Business Profile | Map visibility, reviews, calls, directions, current photos, local credibility |
| Ad platforms | Capture or create demand and route it to the right landing experience |
| CRM/job ledger | Decide what actually worked based on booked work, revenue, margin, repeats, and referrals |

### Launch sitemap

Launch with a small set of genuinely different pages:

```text
/
├── /hinsdale-window-cleaning/
├── /residential-window-cleaning/
├── /commercial-window-cleaning/
├── /gutter-cleaning/
├── /pressure-washing/                 [only after service/brand approval]
├── /projects/                         [index after 3+ project stories]
│   ├── /projects/hinsdale-historic-home-.../
│   ├── /projects/hinsdale-storefront-.../
│   └── /projects/hinsdale-condominium-.../
├── /service-area/
├── /about-dave-and-the-local-team/
├── /reviews/
├── /request-an-estimate/
└── /lp/                               [noindex paid landing pages]
    ├── /hinsdale-residential-windows/
    └── /hinsdale-commercial-windows/
```

The homepage may initially serve as the Hinsdale hub if speed requires it. The separate `/hinsdale-window-cleaning/` page becomes valuable once the homepage must speak to the full cluster or once local search data shows a distinct opportunity.

### Town-page rollout

**At launch:** Hinsdale only, with a strong nearby-area section for Oak Brook, Burr Ridge, Western Springs, and Countryside.

**Next pages:** Oak Brook and Burr Ridge are the first candidates, but only after each has at least:

- three completed jobs or one meaningful commercial relationship;
- original local photos;
- a town-specific testimonial, project detail, or operational insight;
- search or campaign evidence of demand;
- enough unique material that the page is not a word-swapped copy.

Western Springs and Countryside follow the same gate. A town does not earn a page merely because Dave serves it.

## 9. Conversion and lead handling

### Estimate form

Keep the first step short:

- name;
- phone;
- email;
- service address or ZIP;
- residential / storefront / commercial;
- service needed;
- preferred contact method;
- optional photos;
- consent language.

Ask building size, pane count, frequency, access issues, and timing later or conditionally. A long universal form will depress conversion and still produce incomplete information.

### Two distinct funnels

**Residential**

- emotional outcome: brighter, cared-for home without the hassle or risk;
- conversion: quick estimate request, call, or photo-assisted inquiry;
- cross-sell: gutters and approved exterior services;
- lifecycle: spring/fall reminders, repeat plan, review, referral, neighborhood follow-up.

**Commercial**

- operational outcome: reliably clean glass without disrupting customers or staff;
- conversion: site walk or commercial quote;
- qualification: property type, locations, frontage, frequency, access, decision timeline;
- lifecycle: recurring route schedule, multi-location expansion, adjacent-business referral.

### Response standard

Choose and publish a response promise only after staffing supports it. Internally, aim for:

- instant confirmation;
- call/text within five minutes during staffed hours for paid leads;
- same-business-day response otherwise;
- at least six contact attempts across call, text, and email over seven days;
- estimate follow-up at 1, 3, 7, and 14 days unless the prospect declines.

The exact cadence can change, but unworked leads cannot be blamed on media.

## 10. Paid acquisition plan

### Order of operations

1. **Past-customer reactivation and referral asks** — lowest-cost demand and immediate learning.
2. **Google Local Services Ads**, if the exact category/service is eligible and approved.
3. **Google Search Ads** — tightly controlled high-intent tests.
4. **Neighborhood follow-up** around completed jobs—door hangers/direct mail/Nextdoor where permitted.
5. **Meta retargeting and proof amplification** after enough local creative and site traffic exist.
6. **Prospecting social** only after the offer and lead handling work.

### Google Search structure

Do not blend all work into one campaign.

**Campaign A — residential windows, Hinsdale cluster**

- narrow geography: presence in target ZIPs, not “interest in” the area;
- exact/phrase high-intent terms first;
- dedicated residential landing page;
- calls and estimate requests as primary actions;
- negative keywords for jobs, DIY, supplies, salary, training, and unrelated auto/glass terms.

**Campaign B — commercial/storefront**

- separate keywords, copy, form, budget, and follow-up;
- target storefront/office/property-manager intent;
- optimize toward qualified site walks and contracts, not raw lead count.

**Campaign C — gutters or approved exterior service**

- launch seasonally and independently;
- do not let a cheap gutter lead hide an expensive window-acquisition problem.

**Brand campaign**

- isolate searches for “FISH Window Cleaning” so branded demand does not make nonbrand acquisition look better than it is;
- investigate whether corporate already covers this demand before paying twice.

### Initial pilot logic

The earlier $750/month test was too small to support multiple broad campaign types plus management overhead. A new pilot should either:

- concentrate the full media budget on one customer type and the five-town cluster; or
- run short sequential tests, not simultaneous underfunded campaigns.

Each test needs a precommitted decision rule:

- pause a keyword/ad group when it has spent the allowable customer-acquisition cost without a qualified opportunity;
- do not scale from a form fill alone;
- scale only after booked jobs show acceptable contribution margin;
- inspect ZIP, service, customer type, search term, lead quality, and close rate weekly.

### Allowable acquisition cost

Set this by customer type:

```text
Allowable CAC = expected first-year gross profit
                × acceptable acquisition percentage
                + expected referral/repeat value adjustment
```

Do not use one CAC target for a one-time small residential job, a recurring storefront, and a large commercial contract. Dave explicitly called out this economic difference; the reporting and bidding structure must preserve it.

## 12. Social: useful, but in the correct role

Social is not the first engine for people actively searching for a window cleaner. Its highest-value jobs are:

- show that the local team is real and active;
- create familiarity before a search or referral;
- retarget visitors who did not request an estimate;
- amplify neighborhood proof around current jobs;
- support recruiting and community relationships;
- create reusable ad and landing-page creative.

### Content mix

- **40% proof:** before/after, project detail, review, repeat client, route day.
- **25% education:** what affects an estimate, interior prep, seasonal timing, historic-window care.
- **20% people/community:** Dave, crew, local partnership, volunteer work, customer thank-you.
- **15% offer/availability:** route opening, seasonal bundle, commercial schedule opening.

### Platform roles

- **Facebook/Instagram:** local proof, retargeting, homeowner reach, creative testing.
- **Nextdoor:** neighbor recommendations and job-adjacent visibility; participate authentically, not with repetitive promotional posts.
- **LinkedIn:** selectively for property managers, facility contacts, local commercial relationships, and completed commercial work.
- **TikTok/short video:** only if the team can produce satisfying, real job footage efficiently; it is a content format, not a launch priority.

### The non-obvious social play

Build content around **route density**, not vanity reach:

> “Our crew will be in Hinsdale near [general neighborhood/landmark] on Thursday; we have one nearby estimate slot.”

Use privacy-safe geography, never a customer's address. The goal is to turn one scheduled job into nearby work with lower travel and acquisition cost.

## 14. Measurement design

### Required systems

- Dave-controlled Google Ads and Meta access;
- GA4 and Google Tag Manager, owned by Dave/the business;
- Search Console;
- approved call tracking with dynamic number insertion while preserving the canonical phone number in crawlable business data;
- CRM or a disciplined lead/job ledger;
- UTM standards;
- offline conversion uploads where technically available;
- recorded lead-source question as a secondary—not sole—source signal.

### Required fields for every opportunity

- created date/time;
- name/contact;
- ZIP/town;
- residential, retail storefront, or commercial;
- service requested;
- source, medium, campaign, ad/keyword or referral detail;
- new vs. existing customer;
- qualified/unqualified and reason;
- estimate amount;
- won/lost/pending and loss reason;
- booked date and service date;
- collected revenue;
- estimated labor/material cost or gross margin;
- repeat plan;
- review requested/received;
- referral or adjacent job created.

### Weekly owner dashboard

One page, plain English:

| Stage | Measures |
|---|---|
| Spend | Media + management + creative/tracking cost |
| Demand | Calls, forms, messages, by ZIP/service/customer type |
| Quality | Qualified opportunities and qualification rate |
| Sales | Estimates, bookings, close rate, average booked value |
| Economics | CAC per booked customer, collected revenue, gross profit, ROAS and profit after marketing |
| Compounding | Repeat bookings, recurring contracts, reviews, referrals, nearby jobs |

The report should include a short decision section: **what changed, why, what we are doing next.** A dashboard that Dave must debug is not a useful deliverable.

## 17. The strategic bottom line

The advantage is not a clever domain, more pages, or a prettier version of the corporate site.

It is a local learning loop that corporate marketing cannot easily reproduce:

```text
real Hinsdale job
    → original proof
    → stronger landing page and ad
    → attributable estimate
    → profitable booked customer
    → review / repeat / referral
    → denser route and better local proof
```

The site is the conversion and evidence layer inside that loop. The advertising buys the first opportunities. Dave's local relationships, service quality, and follow-through make the economics compound.

That is the north star: not becoming a large generic home-services marketer, but becoming the most credible, measurable, and operationally dense window-cleaning choice in Hinsdale and the surrounding cluster.
