## FISH Window Cleaning — Dave Weishar, Chicago Western & Southern Suburbs

**Prepared:** September 20, 2026  
**Decision standard:** What we would actually implement if we were accountable for Dave's booked gross profit—not traffic, impressions, pages, or marketing activity.

---

## 1. The answer

Build a **Hinsdale-led acquisition system**, not a generic local-services website.

The system has three distinct growth motions:

1. **Premium residential:** capture active demand in Hinsdale, prove the unit economics, then expand into Burr Ridge, Western Springs, and Oak Brook.
2. **Neighborhood commercial:** grow recurring storefront, restaurant, office, community-facility, and low-rise association routes through proof, direct outreach, referrals, and tightly limited search.
3. **Installed-base growth:** reactivate old customers, cross-sell appropriate services, pre-book the next visit, request honest reviews, earn referrals, and convert each job into nearby work.

The website supports these motions. It is not the motion by itself.

### North star

> **Twelve-month contribution profit from newly acquired target-area customers, including repeat, referral, and same-neighborhood revenue.**

Track residential and commercial separately.

Operational companion metric:

> **Booked contribution profit per available crew-hour, by source and town.**

This prevents a high-revenue job with excessive travel or labor from looking better than a dense, repeatable route.

## 2. The strategic diagnosis

Dave does not primarily have a “website problem.” He has an **information and control problem**:

- FISH corporate controls the current website structure.
- Agency reporting has emphasized impressions, clicks, and high-level channel labels rather than customers and economics.
- Brand and nonbrand traffic may be blended.
- Residential, storefront, and larger commercial leads have different economics but have been discussed as one funnel.
- Dave cannot currently connect each dollar spent to a qualified lead, estimate, completed job, gross profit, repeat visit, or referral.
- The strongest proof—real Hinsdale customers and jobs—is mostly invisible.
- The purchased domain creates franchise, trademark, and territory risk before it creates value.

The solution is to create a local measurement and proof layer Dave controls, while preserving one legitimate FISH business identity.

## 3. The first hard decision: do not launch `fishchicagoland.com`

Treat the purchased domain as parked.

Why:

1. The publicly available FISH franchise documents reserve unusually broad control over FISH marks, domains, websites, Internet advertising, social identities, and linking. Dave's executed agreement and current manual—not a public sample—are controlling.
2. FISH already has another official location named **“Chicagoland & Chicago West Suburbs,”** owned by Dereck Jaggers. The proposed domain risks customer, search, and territory confusion: [official location](https://www.fishwindowcleaning.com/Chicagoland-IL-3289/).
3. A new Business Profile or second LSA identity would discard Dave's reviews and risk violating Google's rule that a service-area business should have one accurate profile.
4. Copying FISH branding and customer logos first and asking permission later is an avoidable franchise and endorsement risk.

### Preferred authorization ladder

1. **Best:** corporate approves a Dave-controlled landing-page system on an official FISH subdomain or approved corporate path, with Dave controlling measurement and experiments.
2. **Second:** written approval for one standalone domain, exact brand treatment, services, tracking, ads, offers, customer proof, ownership, and termination/transfer rules.
3. **Third:** written approval for a neutral local domain that transparently represents Dave's independently operated FISH franchise.
4. **Do not do:** FISH-branded domain without permission, town domains, town subdomains, cloned corporate pages, or a second Google profile.

Approval should also resolve campaign overlap: who may bid on brand/nonbrand terms, in which ZIPs, using which destination, with which conversion definitions.

### What can start while approval is pending

- customer/job data audit;
- customer interviews;
- proof permissions and photography;
- reactivation and referral programs using approved channels;
- lead-handling improvements;
- current Google Ads/LSA/GBP audit;
- LSA using the existing legitimate business identity, if corporate and account rules permit;
- ad tests to the existing approved page or Google-hosted lead experience;
- wireframes and copy in staging, not public deployment.

## 4. The market we are choosing

The five towns are not interchangeable.

| Market | Role | Positioning | Acquisition priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hinsdale** | Spearhead | Trusted inside valuable homes; historic/divided-light window care; strong local business proof; precise scope | First |
| **Burr Ridge** | Large-property expansion | Multi-story/custom homes; house + garage; windows + gutters; repeat seasonal care | Second, after local proof |
| **Western Springs** | Community-trust expansion | Named local accountability, careful in-home protocol, relationships, referrals | Second/third, after proof |
| **Oak Brook residential** | Premium estate motion | Privacy, property protection, scheduled seasonal care | Separate residential test |
| **Oak Brook commercial** | Office/retail/association motion | Insurance, dependable schedule, site walk, recurring invoicing | Separate commercial motion |
| **Countryside** | Route-fill market | Exterior convenience, bundles, neighbor scheduling, existing-customer growth | Do not lead cold-media expansion |

Hinsdale's public fact sheet reports a median home value around $875,900 and a very high share of $200,000+ households. Burr Ridge and Western Springs also show high home values, household income, and owner occupancy. These facts support a premium trust/care proposition; they do not prove demand or allowable acquisition cost.

### Customer segments

**Residential priority**

- homeowners who value care, reliability, and convenience over the lowest price;
- historic/renovated homes, divided-light windows, larger homes, condominiums;
- households likely to buy inside + outside, gutters, or repeat seasonal service.

**Commercial priority**

- storefronts and restaurants;
- professional offices and showrooms;
- community facilities;
- low-rise condominium/HOA properties;
- multi-location local operators where capability is proven.

Exclude complex high-rise/rope-access work unless Dave has the certifications, crews, insurance, and desire to compete with dedicated commercial specialists.

## 5. The positioning and product

### Market position

> **Local accountability with professional systems.**

The defensible combination is:

- Dave and a real local team;
- specific Hinsdale-area experience;
- careful in-home behavior;
- clear written scope and price;
- professional FISH operating standards;
- fast, human communication;
- proof from actual nearby work.

The category does not need another “streak-free satisfaction” claim. It needs certainty.

### Core promise

> **A clear quote, careful work inside your home, and no surprises on service day.**

Only after operations confirm the details, productize that as:

- written scope before scheduling;
- glass, screens, sills, tracks, storms, and add-ons shown separately;
- no additional work or price change without approval;
- arrival confirmation and on-the-way text;
- shoe covers and floor/furniture protection;
- final walkthrough;
- defined touch-up/rain policy;
- option to reserve the next seasonal visit.

### Residential offer

**Clear View Home Service**

- Exterior Refresh
- Inside + Out
- Whole-Home Detail

These are scope choices, not artificial “good/better/best” discounts. Exact inclusions and pricing come from Dave's last 50–100 invoices.

Two retention tests:

1. **Clear View Twice-Yearly:** spring inside + out, fall exterior refresh, priority scheduling, 12-month price clarity subject to unchanged scope.
2. **Windows + Fall Gutters:** same-visit bundle where reduced setup/travel creates real savings and margin remains acceptable.

### Commercial offer

**Hinsdale Business Care**

- one-time reset, then weekly/biweekly/monthly/quarterly routes;
- agreed service window, including off-hours when operationally possible;
- certificate of insurance and W-9 ready;
- one point of contact;
- itemized predictable invoicing;
- completion/photo confirmation where useful;
- multi-location option only if true.

The CTA is **Request a site walk**, not “Get a homeowner coupon.”

## 8. Customer and job intelligence sprint

No amount of Internet research can reveal Dave's margins, close rate, repeat behavior, crew capacity, or why his customers chose him. These are not optional missing inputs; they determine the media plan.

### Data pull

Export 24 months of:

- customer and estimate date;
- ZIP/town;
- residential / storefront / larger commercial;
- service and scope;
- quoted, booked, completed, collected revenue;
- labor hours and variable cost proxy;
- source;
- lost reason;
- repeat visits;
- next appointment;
- review/referral/nearby job where known.

Analyze:

- contribution per crew-hour;
- average ticket and contribution margin;
- lead → qualified → estimate → booked → completed rates;
- repeat and cross-sell rates;
- profitability by ZIP, service, customer type, and route;
- seasonality by week;
- unsold-estimate and lapsed-customer opportunity.

### Interviews

Interview 10 recent wins, 5 lost estimates, and 5 recurring/commercial customers. Ask:

1. What triggered the search that day?
2. What worried you most?
3. Where did you look and what did you type or ask?
4. Who else did you consider?
5. What made you contact and choose FISH?
6. What information reduced risk?
7. What nearly stopped the booking?
8. How did you decide the price was fair?
9. What did the crew do that felt professional?
10. What would make repeat scheduling worthwhile?
11. For lost estimates: what won instead?
12. For commercial: which failure causes the most pain?

Use the customers' exact language to write the page and ads.

## 9. Economics and budget

### Core formulas

```text
24-month contribution LTV
= revenue per visit × contribution margin × expected visits
+ measured add-on contribution
+ measured referral/nearby-job contribution

Maximum CAC
= contribution LTV × acceptable acquisition share

Maximum raw-lead CPL
= maximum CAC × raw-lead-to-completed-customer rate
```

Set add-on/referral value to zero until measured.

### External benchmark, used only as a prior

LocaliQ's 2025 U.S. home-services Search dataset reported window cleaning around **$9.12 CPC, 13.58% website conversion rate, and $66.69 raw-lead CPL**. It is not a Chicago quote, qualified-lead cost, or booked-customer cost. A cautious planning range for affluent-suburb Search is **$10–$18 CPC and $85–$150 per raw lead** until Keyword Planner and live results replace it. [Benchmark report](https://www.wordstream.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/ws-guide-home-services-benchmarks-2025.pdf)

### Two responsible launch paths

**Minimum viable learning — recommended for Dave initially**

- one channel: verified LSA/pay-per-lead **or** nonbrand Search;
- one segment: Hinsdale-cluster residential windows;
- approximately $1,500–$2,500 monthly media, adjusted to live forecast and capacity;
- run until roughly 20–30 qualified leads or an obvious failure condition;
- commercial handled through route/outbound, not starved paid campaigns.

**Faster comparative learning**

- LSA: $2,250–$3,000;
- nonbrand residential Search: $1,750–$2,250;
- commercial Search: $300–$500;
- brand defense: $150–$250 after corporate overlap audit;
- total: roughly $4,450–$6,000 monthly for 8–12 weeks.

Only choose the second path if Dave can fulfill 25–35 incremental jobs monthly and the modeled CAC is supportable. An agency's desire for faster data does not justify uneconomic spend.

## 10. Paid acquisition implementation

### Channel sequence

1. Existing-customer reactivation and unsold-estimate recovery.
2. LSA/pay-per-lead using the current verified identity, if eligible and approved.
3. Tightly controlled Google Search.
4. Route-day and five-around neighborhood acquisition.
5. Retargeting using real proof.
6. Cold Meta only after proof, offer, tracking, and follow-up work.

Google currently lists window cleaning as LSA-eligible. LSA is migrating for some advertisers into pay-per-lead Performance Max during 2026–27, so export existing reports before any migration. Ranking considers relevance, reviews, response time, missed calls, profile completeness, imagery, and budget: [eligibility](https://ads.google.com/intl/en_us/intl/en_us/home/local-services-ads/), [ranking factors](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305), [migration](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17213585).

### Search campaigns

1. Brand defense — only after corporate keyword/destination audit.
2. Residential windows — core.
3. Gutters — seasonal, separate economics.
4. Pressure washing — only after approval and profit validation.
5. Commercial windows — small, separate conversion definition.

Do not split learning campaigns by every town. Use town/ZIP targeting, page proof, ad copy, and reporting; split only when economics justify it.

### Initial search intent

- window cleaning near me
- window cleaning service
- residential window cleaning
- house window cleaning
- window cleaners/window washers near me
- window cleaning/window washing Hinsdale
- commercial/storefront/office window cleaning

Start exact and phrase. Mine real search terms before broad expansion. Set geography to **Presence: people in or regularly in the targeted locations**, not Google's broader default interest setting. Turn Search Partners off initially.

### Initial negatives

- job/career/salary/training/course;
- DIY/how-to/supplies/tools/squeegee/machine/rental;
- car/auto/windshield/tint;
- replacement/installation/repair/doors;
- maid/housekeeper/dry cleaning;
- gutter installation/guards/repair if not offered;
- pressure-washer purchase/rental;
- high-rise/rope access if excluded;
- towns outside authorized territory.

Do not automatically exclude price/cost/cheap; measure their actual customer quality.

### Bidding progression

1. Validate calls, forms, CRM IDs, and test leads first.
2. Begin with controlled exact/phrase traffic and Maximize Clicks with a ceiling or Maximize Conversions only if reliable prior data exists.
3. Import qualified-lead outcomes daily.
4. Promote Qualified Lead to primary after a 2–3 week validation period.
5. Move toward booked/completed contribution value only when volume and upload reliability support it.

Google's current lead-measurement system supports hashed first-party data and offline outcomes through enhanced conversions. Do not bid on page views, button clicks, every short call, or raw forms forever: [Google enhanced conversions](https://business.google.com/us/accelerate/announcements/enhanced-conversions/).

## 12. Lead handling and measurement

### Service-level target

Baseline the current process for one week. Then adopt the fastest standard the team can keep:

- answer live when possible;
- immediate automated acknowledgement;
- human response within five minutes during staffed hours;
- same-business-day otherwise;
- structured estimate follow-up;
- LSA messages/booking enabled only when someone owns them.

Google explicitly uses LSA responsiveness and missed calls in ranking.

### CRM stages

New → Contacted → Qualified → Estimate scheduled/sent → Booked → Completed → Lost reason → Spam/duplicate.

Required loss reasons: unreachable, outside area, wrong service, price, timing, competitor, capacity.

### Required capture

- click IDs and UTMs;
- landing page/campaign/ad group/keyword/search term where available;
- phone/form/text;
- ZIP/town and service;
- customer type;
- new/existing;
- response time;
- qualified reason;
- estimate value;
- booked/completed/collected revenue;
- variable cost or contribution estimate;
- repeat, review, referral, and nearby job.

### Weekly scorecard

| Funnel | Business | Compounding | Operations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spend, raw leads, qualified leads, estimates | Bookings, collected revenue, contribution, CAC, contribution ROAS | Repeat, plan enrollment, bundle rate, reviews, referrals, nearby jobs | Response time, missed calls, quote SLA, close rate, crew-hours, route miles |

Report by source, customer type, service, and ZIP—not just totals.

## 13. Experiment cards

Run one major variable at a time. With low volume, use sequential 2–4 week cohorts and avoid declaring winners from five leads.

| Test | Hypothesis | Primary decision metric | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Written quote vs. text photos CTA | Photo path reduces friction and site-visit cost | Qualified-lead rate | Book rate and job quality |
| Care/reliability vs. speed hero | In-home trust matters more than generic speed | Cost/booked customer | Response SLA truthfulness |
| Real project above fold | Specific local proof increases trust | Lead→book rate | Page conversion |
| Price examples vs. no anchor | Scope transparency improves qualification | Cost/completed customer | Average ticket, margin |
| Windows + gutters | Route/setup efficiency can fund a bundle | Contribution/job | Attach rate, crew time |
| Price lock/priority vs. discount care plan | Certainty beats coupons in premium towns | 12-month plan contribution | Churn/cancellation |
| Real route-day message | Legitimate scarcity creates dense bookings | Contribution/crew-hour | Customer experience |

### Kill rules

Pause/correct immediately when tracking fails, geography is wrong, calls go unanswered, or claims are unsupported.

At keyword/ad-group level:

- revise/pause near **2× allowable qualified-lead CPL** with no qualified lead;
- revise/pause near **2× allowable CAC** with no booked customer, allowing for lag;
- pause when clearly irrelevant spend exceeds 15–20% of reviewed spend.

### Scale rules

Increase budget only when:

- at least ten recent completed customers are attributable;
- cost/completed customer is at or below 80% of cap;
- contribution payback is acceptable;
- response and fulfillment SLAs hold;
- marginal ZIPs remain profitable.

Increase roughly 15–20% weekly, not by doubling.

## 14. Social, local media, and SEO

### Social

Use Facebook/Instagram as **proof distribution and retargeting**, not the first acquisition wedge:

- real jobs and before/after;
- team familiarity;
- old-window/property-care demonstrations;
- local business spotlights with permission;
- seasonal reminders and route openings;
- retargeting visitors and consented customer audiences.

Test cold Meta only after the site, proof, attribution, and follow-up work. Judge completed-customer economics, not cheap instant-form leads.

Use Nextdoor as recommendation/referral infrastructure. Use LinkedIn for property-manager/account research, not as the first direct-response channel.

### Local partnerships/media

Hinsdale Chamber, Western Springs Business Association, local publications, realtors, builders, designers, HOA/property managers, and community groups can create trust and commercial introductions. Treat every paid placement as a trackable experiment with a unique URL/number and matched-ZIP analysis. Sponsorship is not automatically performance marketing.

### SEO

The compounding content unit is a real project:

```text
completed job
→ photos + facts + permission
→ project page
→ town/service page proof
→ GBP/social/review asset
→ higher conversion and stronger entity evidence
```

Do not publish generic AI blog posts, town matrices, fake projects, landmark prose, or customer images taken from the web.

## 15. Ninety-day implementation

### Days 1–10 — truth, access, authority

- review executed franchise agreement/manual and obtain written digital approval;
- export all existing LSA/Ads history before platform migration;
- inventory ownership/access: GBP, Ads, LSA, GA4, GTM, Search Console, Meta, phone tracking, CRM;
- obtain corporate campaigns, terms, geographies, spend, conversions, and destination URLs;
- pull 24-month job/estimate data;
- baseline response time and missed calls;
- conduct first customer interviews;
- confirm exact NAP and service list;
- request permission from 5–7 ideal local customers.

**Exit gate:** authorized path, economics model, account map, approved/unsupported claim sheet.

### Days 11–25 — product and measurement

- define residential/commercial scope, exclusions, price logic, arrival protocol, touch-up policy;
- photograph Dave/team and three representative jobs;
- build conversion core and two noindex landing pages in staging;
- implement call/form/text attribution and CRM stages;
- configure qualified/booked/completed outcome imports;
- write response and estimate follow-up templates;
- QA mobile, speed, accessibility, consent, events, and routing.

**Exit gate:** every CTA works; test leads reconcile to CRM; response dry run passes; three proof assets exist.

### Days 26–45 — controlled launch

- publish only after authorization;
- launch reactivation and unsold-estimate recovery;
- activate/optimize LSA or one narrow Search campaign;
- launch Hinsdale residential landing experience;
- publish three project pages;
- run consistent non-incentivized review requests;
- start commercial route mapping and ten-account outreach.

**Exit gate:** real leads are traceable through qualification and estimate; search terms and calls are reviewed twice weekly.

### Days 46–65 — improve economics

- analyze by ZIP, service, response time, query, and customer type;
- add negatives and stop bad patterns;
- test CTA or proof placement;
- test price examples only after invoice analysis;
- pilot same-visit window/gutter bundle;
- run one real route-day campaign;
- publish one project/week.

### Days 66–90 — retention and expansion

- pilot twice-yearly plan to existing satisfied customers;
- complete commercial customer/prospect interviews;
- deepen commercial proof and procurement sheet;
- add one town only if it passes the evidence gate;
- decide whether economics justify the dual-channel media path;
- allocate next-quarter budget by completed contribution, not platform CPL.
