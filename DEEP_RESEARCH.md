# Deep Research: Building a Local Growth Engine for FISH Window Cleaning

_Chicago western and southern suburbs — September 20, 2026_

## The answer in one page

The winning strategy is not “make a page for every town.” It is to build a closed-loop local growth engine:

```text
Capture active demand (Google LSA + Search)
        ↓
Convert with local proof, quote clarity, and fast response
        ↓
Complete a profitable job
        ↓
Create a review, photos, project proof, referral, and next service date
        ↓
Use that proof to win nearby customers and increase route density
        ↓
Feed completed-job economics back into ads and page expansion
```

The North Star should be:

> **Incremental contribution margin per available crew hour attributable to marketing.**

That is better than lead volume or even ROAS because a $500 job beside two existing customers can be more valuable than a $600 job that creates ninety minutes of travel. The core diagnostics are completed-job CAC, contribution ROAS, repeat contribution, and travel/crew hours.

The key decisions are:

1. **Do not publicly use `fishchicagoland.com` until the executed franchise agreement is reviewed and written approval is obtained.** The current FISH agreement form appears to prohibit exactly this.
2. If approved, build **one domain with subdirectories**. Do not use a domain per town or a subdomain per town.
3. Launch approximately **15 strong pages**, not 100 generated ones: core services, five town hubs, trust/conversion pages, and real project pages.
4. Use **Google LSA and tightly controlled Search** for immediate demand. The site is valuable for conversion and measurement, but it is not necessary to start LSA.
5. Use social primarily as a **local-proof and demand-creation system**, not a daily content treadmill.
6. Build the moat around **operational certainty + local proof + careful property treatment**, not lowest price.

## 1. A critical constraint the first pass understated

### The public 2025 FISH agreement form is explicit

The publicly available [2025 FISH Franchise Disclosure Document and sample Franchise Agreement](https://www.franchimp.com/pdfjs/read.php?f=104247_2025.pdf) says, among other things:

- FISH has the sole right to market using its marks on the Internet, including websites, domains, URLs, ads, social media, linking, and co-branding.
- A franchisee may not register a domain containing the marks or market online using them without prior written approval.
- The sample agreement states that a franchisee may not separately register a domain containing the marks or operate a website for the business; the franchisor reserves approval rights over any business website.
- It describes registration of a domain containing the marks as possible grounds for immediate termination.
- Its telephone/Internet schedule assigns mark-bearing domains, social identities, and related listings to the franchisor and provides transfer rights at termination.

The operative language appears around pages 33, 82, 99, and 118–119 of the PDF. The public FISH site also separately restricts copying corporate content and using FISH marks without permission in its [Terms of Use](https://www.fishwindowcleaning.com/Chicagoland-IL-3289/terms-of-use).

This public form may not be the exact agreement governing Dave's franchise. The executed agreement, renewals, amendments, territory schedule, operating manual, approvals, and applicable law control. But the strategic conclusion is unavoidable:

> **Do not connect, publish, advertise, or put FISH branding on `fishchicagoland.com` until written authorization is in hand.**

This also means that simply choosing a neutral domain may not solve the problem—the sample agreement appears to reserve approval over operating any website for the franchised business.

### There is also a territory/identity collision

FISH already has another official location branded [“Chicagoland & Chicago West Suburbs”](https://www.fishwindowcleaning.com/Chicagoland-IL-3289/). Using “Fish Chicagoland” could confuse customers, Google, and another franchise territory even if the trademark issue were resolved.

### The correct approval request

Ask FISH for a written exception or pilot approval that specifies:

- the exact domain;
- approved brand name, logo, colors, claims, disclosures, photos, testimonials, and copy;
- approved towns/ZIPs and services;
- whether it may be indexed organically;
- whether Google/Meta/Nextdoor ads may point to it;
- whether it may collect leads or must send to FISH's corporate form;
- lead-data ownership, privacy, security, and reporting;
- analytics, pixels, call tracking, and offline-conversion uploads;
- account ownership and what transfers on termination;
- the creative/offer approval process;
- whether the corporate location page will link to it.

While approval is pending, immediate acquisition can use the existing authorized location and service pages, the existing GBP, Google-hosted lead forms/calls, and LSA.

This is an organic-web teardown, not a definitive Maps rank grid. Local Pack and LSA results change by precise searcher location and should later be measured from points inside the territory.

### The strongest local organic competitor: My Window Washing

[My Window Washing's Hinsdale page](https://www.mywindowwashing.com/hinsdale) is the most complete local page found. It combines:

- local review count and rating;
- one-hour response claim;
- a realistic local price range;
- Hinsdale-specific home types and window problems;
- service scope and guarantees;
- a named nearby dispatch office;
- town-specific FAQs;
- related services and nearby towns;

Its [Burr Ridge page](https://www.mywindowwashing.com/burr-ridge) is meaningfully different: large custom estates, coach houses, wooded lots, separate garage gutters, and bundled main-house/garage service. That is genuine intent matching, not only swapping a city token.

Its most interesting SEO mechanism is the [individual project page](https://www.mywindowwashing.com/projects/gutter-cleaning-in-hinsdale-08-17-26): date, town, house type, service scope, price, crew, photos/video, and related local links. Each completed job becomes original content, long-tail relevance, sales proof, and material for other channels.

Weaknesses we can exploit:

- very long and visibly templated pages;
- “lowest/fair price” positioning that commoditizes the work;
- some thin “coming soon” project sections;
- an opportunity for FISH to appear more premium, concise, careful, and operationally mature.

## 3. The position to own

Do not compete as the cheapest cleaner. Own:

> **The western and southern suburbs' most dependable, careful window-care team: fast, clear quotes; background-checked uniformed crews; precise arrival communication; white-glove property protection; and documented results.**

The three-part moat:

### Local proof

- real crew;
- real town;
- real property type;
- real service and date;
- real photos;
- real attributed review;
- real result.

### Operational certainty

- response-time target;
- written scope before work;
- arrival window and on-the-way text;
- no surprise add-ons;
- final walkthrough;
- photo proof where work is hard to inspect;
- clearly stated weather/touch-up policy.

### Care inside and outside the property

- background-checked uniformed technicians;
- shoe covers and drop cloths;
- protection for floors, furniture, landscaping, historic trim and specialty glass;
- capability with storms, divided lights, high windows, atriums, skylights and estates;
- insured/bonded trust.

Subject to FISH approval, package this as a named operating promise such as “The Clear-Care Standard.” A named promise is more memorable than a generic satisfaction statement, but it must reflect practices the operation will consistently deliver.

## 4. Domain and site architecture

### Ranked recommendation

1. **Best:** FISH adds approved town/landing pages inside Dave's existing location path on `fishwindowcleaning.com`.
2. **Second-best:** FISH gives written approval for one branded standalone domain and links to it from the official location page.
3. **Acceptable if explicitly approved:** one neutral standalone domain that transparently represents the FISH franchise and does not create a second misleading business identity.
4. **Do not do:** one domain per town, a subdomain per town, or many thin microsites.

The fact that option 1 may be politically difficult does not make options 2 or 3 safe without approval.

### Why one domain and folders

Google says it has no ranking preference between subdomains and subdirectories; choose the structure easiest to manage. One domain lets links, mentions, projects, content, and engagement support one property. [Google crawling/indexing FAQ](https://developers.google.com/search/help/crawling-index-faq)

Exact-match domains are not a shortcut. Google says keywords in a domain or URL alone have hardly any ranking effect and operates an exact-match-domain system to avoid over-rewarding them. [Google SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide), [ranking systems guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide)

Multiple near-identical city sites/pages are a known doorway-abuse pattern. Google specifically calls out multiple domains or pages targeting cities that funnel to the same destination. [Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)

There is no officially documented “new-domain sandbox,” but a new site begins without inbound links, history, or established entity signals. Splitting that cold start across many properties is self-defeating.

### Recommended URL structure

```text
/
├── residential-window-cleaning/
├── commercial-window-cleaning/
│   ├── storefronts/
│   ├── offices-medical/
│   ├── restaurants/
│   └── property-managers-hoas/
├── gutter-cleaning/
├── pressure-washing/
├── pricing/
├── care-plans/
├── service-areas/
│   ├── hinsdale-il/
│   ├── burr-ridge-il/
│   ├── oak-brook-il/
│   ├── downers-grove-il/
│   └── oak-lawn-il/          ← validate with job economics
├── projects/
│   ├── hinsdale-historic-home-window-cleaning/
│   ├── burr-ridge-estate-window-and-gutter-service/
│   └── oak-brook-storefront-window-cleaning/
├── about/
├── reviews/
└── estimate/
```

Ad-only duplicates or narrow experiments can live below `/go/` and be `noindex` when they do not add organic value.

### Launch tier: roughly 15 pages

| Residential window cleaning | Convert homeowners | Interior care, screens/tracks/sills, storms, high windows, pets/access, process |
| Commercial window cleaning | Convert business buyers | Recurring reliability, COI, off-hours, storefront cadence, multi-site billing |
| Gutter cleaning | Capture seasonal demand and bundles | Safety, downspouts, photo proof, tree cover, fall timing |
| Pressure washing or best approved add-on | Cross-sell and widen demand | Publish only if operationally meaningful and approved |
| Pricing / how estimates work | Reduce uncertainty | Typical ranges or starting point, inclusions, quote variables, minimum if applicable |
| Care plans | Create recurring revenue | Spring/fall, priority scheduling, locked/clear pricing, add-on logic |
| About Dave and the local team | Humanize national brand | Local tenure, crew standards, community ties, authentic photos |
| Reviews/customer stories | Consolidate proof | Attributed and permissioned, segmented by residential/commercial/town |
| Estimate | Convert | Short form, ZIP, service, timing, call/text preference, optional photo upload |
| Service-area hub | Explain real territory | Honest towns/ZIPs, no fake offices |
| Hinsdale | Win premium historic/estate intent | Original/divided-light/storm windows, tree cover, careful interiors |
| Burr Ridge | Win estate/bundle intent | Large homes, atriums, coach houses, separate garage gutters |
| Oak Brook | Win estate plus commercial intent | Executive homes, offices, retail/storefronts, recurring programs |
| Downers Grove | Win mixed-housing intent | Historic homes, ranches, multi-unit and practical service options |
| One southern-market hub | Avoid a west-only strategy | Choose Oak Lawn, Flossmoor, or another town from actual margin/route data |

Publish three to six real project pages as soon as proof is available. They are more valuable than generic blog posts.

### Do not launch the full service × town grid

Create an intersection such as `/service-areas/oak-brook-il/commercial-window-cleaning/` only when:

- Search/Ads data shows distinct demand;
- the service matters economically;
- real local jobs/photos/knowledge exist;
- the page answers meaningfully different questions;
- the business can maintain it.

“Commercial window cleaning in Oak Brook” is a plausible early intersection. “Screen cleaning in all 20 towns” is not.

### Town-page publishing gate

No town page goes live without:

- verified service availability and territory rights;
- at least one genuine local operational insight;
- original local imagery or a completed-job example;
- useful property/service information beyond municipal trivia;
- truthful response/scheduling details;
- a materially different reason for the customer to choose the page.

Automation may build templates, metadata drafts, galleries, breadcrumbs and related-town links. It must not invent local facts, testimonials, jobs, prices, or expertise. Google's scaled-content rule is based on purpose and value, not whether AI was used. [Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies), [people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)

### Town-page anatomy

1. Town + service outcome in the first screen.
2. Tap-to-call plus a short estimate path.
3. Local review and real job proof.
4. What makes properties there different.
5. Services available, linked to canonical service pages.
6. Pricing/estimate education.
7. Real scheduling, route-day, parking or access information.
8. Property-care and crew trust proof.
9. Town-specific FAQs from actual calls.
10. Nearby operationally related towns.

Do not embed a map pin or imply an office in a town where none exists.

## 6. Immediate paid demand strategy

### First: establish closed-loop measurement

Track:

```text
raw lead
→ reachable
→ qualified
→ estimate scheduled
→ estimate delivered
→ booked
→ completed and paid
→ repeat/referral
```

For each lead, store source, campaign, query where available, town/ZIP, service, residential/commercial, quote, outcome, revenue, direct cost, job hours, drive time, repeat date, and loss reason.

The four CACs:

- raw-lead CPL;
- qualified-lead CPL;
- booked-job CAC;
- completed-job CAC.

Only the last two decide scaling. Google recommends qualified/converted lead imports, not merely form fills. [Google enhanced conversions for leads](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/11021502)

### Google Local Services Ads

Window cleaning is currently eligible for LSA in the United States. LSA uses the verified business/profile, services, areas, reviews, responsiveness and Google-hosted lead experience; it does not need the proposed website to begin. [Google LSA eligibility](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841/getting-started-with-local-services-ads-united-states)

Non-obvious leverage: LSA rank is not merely budget. Google cites bid, relevance, responsiveness/missed calls, review quality/count, response time, photos, verification, and message/booking availability. Operations affect both rank and lead cost. [Google LSA ranking factors](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305)

Actions:

- determine whether an LSA account already exists;
- connect the legitimate existing GBP;
- complete every verification requirement;
- select only profitable, approved services and areas;
- answer rapidly and recover missed calls;
- upload real job/team photos;
- request a review after every successful job;
- record downstream job economics;
- dispute invalid leads consistently;
- save current settings/history because Google has begun a 2026–2027 migration of some LSA accounts into Google Ads/PMax pay-per-lead campaigns. [Google migration notice](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17213585)

### Google Search

Audit and obtain access to the agency account before launching overlapping campaigns. Google prohibits attempts to gain unfair auction advantage by showing more than one ad for the same business in the same position. Even when no enforcement occurs, fragmented accounts make attribution and bidding worse. [Google unfair advantage policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15936768)

Initial campaigns:

- brand defense, coordinated with existing activity;
- residential window cleaning;
- commercial/storefront window cleaning;
- gutter cleaning;
- approved high-margin add-ons only after evidence.

Start exact/phrase around high-intent service, “near me,” quote, and town queries. Separate residential and commercial. Group towns into route clusters instead of dozens of starving campaigns.

Immediately use:

- `Presence: people in or regularly in the area`, not the broader default interest setting;
- aggressive negatives for jobs, DIY, supplies, auto glass, repairs/replacement, irrelevant cleaning and out-of-territory towns;
- call reporting;
- location assets linked to the real GBP;
- lead-form assets when appropriate;
- service-matched pages;
- completed-job imports.

Sources: [Google presence-only targeting](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9376662), [location assets](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2404182), [match types](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7478529).

Do not begin with broad match, AI Max or PMax as the main campaign. Test expansion only after clean qualified/completed-job signals, query hygiene, and adequate volume exist. Prevent PMax from harvesting brand demand and claiming it created it.

Directional—not guaranteed—2025 LocaliQ data reported a $66.69 platform CPL for window-cleaning Search versus $90.92 for home services overall. Those are not completed-job numbers. [LocaliQ benchmark](https://localiq.com/blog/home-services-search-advertising-benchmarks/)

## 7. How to leverage social

### Social's real job

Organic social is not the principal lead engine. It is the proof factory and creative testing layer for:

- landing pages;
- GBP;
- paid Meta/Nextdoor;
- retargeting;
- estimate follow-up;
- customer reactivation;
- sales credibility.

### The job-site capture SOP

For every permissioned job, the crew captures:

1. five seconds of the problem/before;
2. five seconds of the process;
3. five seconds of the result/reflection;
4. one crew/vehicle image;
5. town, property type, service, complication, and result;
6. an optional customer comment.

One job becomes:

- a project page;
- one Reel/Short;
- three still ads;
- a GBP update;
- town-page evidence;
- estimate-follow-up proof;
- review request context;
- Nextdoor/Facebook proof where allowed.

This is far more useful than producing generic “five reasons to clean your windows” posts.

### Channel roles

| Channel | Role | Do | Do not |
|---|---|---|---|
| Facebook/Instagram | Create seasonal demand, retarget, reactivate and fill route gaps | Real before/after, owner/crew video, local reviews, route-day and recurring-plan offers | Optimize to cheapest forms or use stock footage |
| Nextdoor | Neighborhood trust, recommendations and route clusters | Claim page, ask real customers for recommendations, share genuine local work, test ZIP-level route ads | Astroturf or spam neighborhood conversations |
| Local Facebook groups | Community credibility | Participate as a real local operator where group rules allow; let customers recommend | Repetitive promotional posting |
| Instagram/TikTok/YouTube Shorts | Reuse transformation footage | Cross-post concise proof | Chase national virality as the KPI |
| LinkedIn | Commercial proof/outbound air cover | Property managers, facilities, offices, retailers, multi-site case studies | Household lead generation |
| GBP | Highest-priority local proof feed | Job photos, accurate services/hours, review engine | Treat it as optional social media |

### Meta campaign design

Test three concepts:

1. **Risk reversal:** rain/touch-up guarantee, subject to operational and franchise approval.
2. **Local transformation:** real town, property and customer proof.
3. **Route-day or care plan:** “Our crew is in Hinsdale Thursday” or spring/fall recurring service.

Test Higher Intent Instant Form against the website/photo-quote page. Qualify with ZIP, residential/commercial, service, approximate scope, timing and contact information. Feed qualified/booked/completed outcomes back to Meta through the permitted CRM/CAPI path. Meta reports materially better quality-lead performance when campaigns optimize with downstream CRM feedback, though its study is platform-supplied evidence. [Meta lead-generation guide](https://about.fb.com/ltam/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2023/11/LeadGenerationGuide.pdf)

### Nextdoor

Nextdoor is unusually aligned with affluent suburban services because recommendations and verified neighborhood context are central to the product. Build the free presence and recommendation base before a paid test. Nextdoor itself reports strong homeowner and recommendation usage; treat platform statistics as directional. [Nextdoor home-services page](https://business.nextdoor.com/en-us/resources/industry/home-garden)

### Marketplace lead sellers

- Claim and complete free Yelp/Nextdoor profiles.
- Test paid Yelp or Thumbtack only with hard caps and completed-job tracking.
- Treat Angi last and demand written pricing, cancellation/refund rules, territory controls and exports. The FTC previously required HomeAdvisor/Angi Leads to pay up to $7.2 million over deceptive claims about lead quality/source. This history does not prove every present lead is bad, but it justifies strict evidence. [FTC HomeAdvisor matter](https://search.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/1923106-homeadvisor-matter)

## 8. Non-obvious plays worth testing

### 1. Route-density bursts

> “We are already servicing three homes in Hinsdale Thursday. Two nearby estimate slots remain.”

Use only when true. Combine Search/Meta/Nextdoor, permitted customer reactivation, and neighbor leave-behinds. This creates real urgency while reducing drive cost.

### 2. Price by operational value, not lead vanity

Allow a higher acquisition cost for a town/ZIP with larger tickets, repeat behavior, strong route density, and available local proof. A lower CPL can be economically worse.

### 3. Schedule-aware media

- raise intent-capture spend when capacity exists;
- reduce it when lead response or scheduling lead time deteriorates;
- use narrow social/Nextdoor campaigns to fill weak route days;
- promote exterior-only service where the customer need not be home;
- move between windows, gutters and commercial routes as seasonality changes.

### 4. Unsold-estimate recovery

Old estimates already have need, property data and territory fit. Run a permissioned 7-day, 30-day and seasonal reactivation program before buying more cold social leads.

### 5. Commercial-to-residential crossover

With permission, feature a recognizable storefront or office job, tag the business, and provide an employee/customer residential offer. The commercial property supplies community proof; residential demand increases the relationship's value.

### 6. Photo quote / quote education

Let prospects upload exterior photos and understand what changes price: window/pane type, stories, access, storms/divided lights, screens/tracks, interior/exterior, and buildup. It reduces the biggest buying uncertainty without promising an inaccurate instant price.

### 7. A recurring care program

Sell a spring/fall plan with automatic reminders, priority scheduling, and optional fall gutters. Retention increases allowable acquisition cost and smooths demand. [Window Hero](https://windowhero.com/hero-club/) and [Window Genie](https://www.windowgenie.com/annual-maintenance-plan/) both demonstrate the category pattern.

### 8. Rain-risk reversal

A clear touch-up guarantee can reduce weather anxiety and cancellations as much as it improves conversion. The operational cost may be low if claims are infrequent, but measure it and get approval.

### 9. Project pages instead of generic blogs

Publish verified project records with customer privacy/permission: town, property type, problem, scope, process, result, crew/time, price range if approved, and photos. These assets serve SEO, paid conversion, referrals, and sales follow-up simultaneously.

### 10. Service-specific review prompts

Ask for an honest review, not a scripted positive one, and make it easy for the customer to mention what was actually performed and where. “The crew carefully cleaned the original storm windows on our Hinsdale Tudor” is far stronger proof than “great job.” Never gate or incentivize reviews. [Google review rules](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)

### 11. Neighborhood follow-through

With permission and local-law/HOA compliance:

- yard sign while the crew is visible;
- door hangers on a narrow adjacent set;
- referral message to the customer;
- EDDM only to proven carrier routes;
- localized retargeting and route-day creative.

USPS permits EDDM selection by carrier route and provides income/age/household filters. [USPS EDDM](https://www.usps.com/business/every-door-direct-mail.htm)

## 9. The 90-day agency plan

### Days 1–10: authority, data and instrumentation

- Review the executed franchise agreement, renewals, brand manual, territory schedule, and current approvals.
- Ask for written website/domain/advertising approval.
- Inventory ownership/admin access for GBP, LSA, Google Ads, GA4, Tag Manager, Search Console, Meta, call tracking, CRM/job system, Yelp and Nextdoor.
- Export 12 months of agency spend, campaigns, keywords/search terms, geographies, calls, forms, conversion definitions and landing pages.
- Export 12–24 months of leads/jobs with town, service, quote, win/loss, revenue, direct cost, crew time, travel time, source and repeat behavior.
- Define qualified lead, booked job, completed job and contribution margin.
- Establish immediate call/missed-call ownership.
- Establish review and job-site proof capture.

### Days 10–30: immediate intent capture

- Start or repair LSA verification and profile completeness.
- Coordinate Search with the existing agency rather than blindly creating a competing account.
- Separate residential, commercial and gutter intent.
- Apply presence-only territory targeting and negatives.
- Fix call measurement and qualified/completed-job imports.
- Use the authorized corporate service pages and Google-hosted lead assets while the new site is gated.
- Begin unsold-estimate and past-customer reactivation where consent allows.

### Days 15–45: private site and proof system

- Build the approved architecture privately.
- Collect real projects, photos, reviews, pricing rules, common questions and route details for five towns.
- Build one source of truth for service/town data.
- Implement analytics, consent/privacy, call/form tracking, spam protection, accessible mobile UX and offline-conversion data.
- Test every lead route before public launch.

### Days 30–60: launch and controlled experiments

- Publish only after approval.
- Launch the core service pages, five town hubs, trust pages and first project pages.
- Point Search traffic to matching pages.
- Test residential landing-page variants.
- Launch three Meta concepts and one Nextdoor route cluster only after follow-up works.
- Compare Instant Form with photo-quote/landing page on qualified and completed outcomes.

### Days 60–90: scale proof, not page count

- Expand towns/services only where contribution economics and real proof justify it.
- Launch the care plan.
- Create project pages from completed jobs.
- Add one commercial vertical/landing page based on pipeline: likely storefronts, property managers/HOAs, or offices/medical.
- Test Yelp/Thumbtack only if core channels have clean measurement.
- Reallocate budget by contribution margin per crew hour.

## 10. What we need to become genuinely data-smart

Research can identify patterns; it cannot tell us Dave's best market without first-party data. The next analysis needs:

1. Executed franchise agreement, amendments and brand/Internet policies.
2. Exact territory map/town/ZIP rights.
3. Current GBP URL/access and review count/history.
4. LSA access/history, if any.
5. Google Ads read/admin access or 12-month export.
6. 12–24 months of leads and completed jobs.
7. Revenue, direct job cost, crew hours and drive time.
8. Residential/commercial split and capacity by week/season.
9. Current customer/estimate lists and consent fields.
10. Photos, testimonials and recognizable commercial customers with permission status.

With those inputs, the next deliverable should be a town × service opportunity model, allowable CACs, actual campaign structure and a prioritized content backlog. Until then, exact budget, town priority and page expansion are informed hypotheses rather than facts.

## Final position

The separate site can be a major asset, but the advantage is not the URL and not the number of pages. It is control of message match, lead capture, measurement, local proof and iteration.
