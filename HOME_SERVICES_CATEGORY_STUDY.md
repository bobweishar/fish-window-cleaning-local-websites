# Home-Services Category Study

**FISH Window Cleaning — Chicago Western & Southern Suburbs**  
**Research date:** September 20, 2026

This is the broader category study missing from the first strategy. It examines how scaled home-service companies structure their websites, local entities, paid acquisition, call handling, recurring revenue, and territory operations. It distinguishes three evidence levels:

- **Verified:** first-party platform documentation or directly observable site structure.
- **Reported:** vendor/operator case study or benchmark; useful but not independently audited.
- **Hypothesis:** a recommendation to test for this franchise.

The objective is not to copy the biggest company. It is to identify which parts of different home-service models apply to window cleaning.

---

## 1. The first strategic distinction: not all home services acquire customers the same way

| Economic archetype | Examples | Customer state | Acquisition engine | Website emphasis | What FISH should borrow |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Emergency repair | Plumbing, HVAC repair, electrical | Urgent; customer often calls the first credible provider | LSA/pay-per-lead, Search, Maps, live phones | 24/7, “near me,” symptom/service pages, immediate booking | Fast call handling, service-specific intent capture, trust |
| High-ticket replacement | Roofing, windows, HVAC replacement, garage doors | Researches, compares, finances | Search, Meta, YouTube, retargeting, nurture | Financing, galleries, long-form proof, estimate funnel | Visual proof, qualification, estimate follow-up |
| Planned maintenance | Pest, lawn care, house cleaning | Known need, but customer can delay and compare | Search/Maps plus seasonal activation | Plans, guarantees, ZIP lookup, recurring value | Recurring plans, seasonal offers, guarantee |
| Discretionary visual care | Window cleaning, pressure washing, detailing | Demand often must be activated | Meta, Nextdoor, direct mail, referrals plus Search | Before/after, convenience, price/quote clarity | Transformation creative, event triggers, route-day offers |
| Commercial route service | Storefront/window/janitorial/lawn routes | Operational buyer values reliability | Search, outbound, referrals, partnerships | Insurance, schedule reliability, account proof | Separate funnel, repeat economics, route density |

### Consequence

Window cleaning should **not** copy an emergency HVAC account wholesale. Emergency companies can tolerate high CPCs because urgency, ticket size, and same-day booking raise the value of a search. Window cleaning wins through a different combination:

1. Capture existing search demand efficiently.
2. Convert a one-time job into repeat service.
3. Raise ticket with sensible bundles.
4. create geographically dense work.
5. Use visual proof to activate latent demand.
6. Reactivate past customers and unsold estimates cheaply.

The economic goal is not maximum lead count. It is:

> **Incremental contribution margin from completed paid jobs per available crew hour, adjusted for travel and repeat value.**

## 2. What scaled operators actually build online

### 2.1 Directly observed architecture patterns

The following are descriptive observations, not proof that the architecture alone caused rankings.

| Operator | Category | Observable structure | What it is doing well | What not to copy blindly |

### 2.1a Additional models that sharpen the lesson

- **[Terminix Chicago](https://www.terminix.com/exterminators/il/chicago-pest-control/)** uses one of the largest observed matrices: state → metro → branch/neighborhood plus service variants. Its branch pages carry real address, phone, hours and managers, while some neighborhood pages sit close to doorway territory because they largely repeat metro content. This is evidence that a matrix can be technically disciplined and still strategically overextended.
- **[Aptive Chicago](https://aptivepestcontrol.com/locations/illinois-il/chicago-pest-control/)** more cleanly separates the metro relevance page from the actual servicing branch. That distinction—market served versus business entity—is exactly what a service-area business must preserve.
- **[Michael & Son Fredericksburg](https://michaelandson.com/fredericksburg)** shows a successful regional operator using shallow market folders rather than a giant location taxonomy. Strong operating footprint, brand and fleet can be the organizing entity.
- **[Bone Dry Indianapolis](https://bonedry.com/indianapolis/)** organizes real market offices with team/review/warranty/financing proof and places commercial roofing on a separate subdomain. That subdomain is a defensible exception because commercial roofing is a distinct business and buyer journey—not because subdomains improve town rankings.
- **[Erie Home Chicago](https://eriehome.com/locations/roofing/chicago-il/)** maps a market label to a real Lombard office and makes financing, warranty and product conversion assets prominent. It also illustrates the need to distinguish national proof from local proof.
- **[Weed Man South Chicago](https://www.weedman.com/en-us/south-chicago)** combines a franchise root, neighborhood pages, recurring programs and an address-based estimator. The quote experience is worth learning from; visibly templated neighborhood pages and location inconsistencies are the governance warning.
- **[Lawn Doctor Cape Cod](https://www.lawndoctor.com/capecod-ma/)** is a good “microsite inside a folder” franchise model: local phone, evaluation, owner-operated positioning, recurring programs and local climate expertise all remain under one root domain.
- **[The Cleaning Authority La Grange](https://www.thecleaningauthority.com/lagrange/about-us/)** pairs a named operating method with real community involvement and an actual office. It is a stronger local trust pattern than generic paragraphs about landmarks.

Across spot checks, rich structured data varied widely. Mr. Rooter, Terminix, Bone Dry and Window Genie exposed comparatively rich entity/service markup; Orkin, Michael & Son, Lawn Doctor and Molly Maid were leaner but still had strong indexable brands and pages. That is useful evidence that schema is hygiene, not the growth engine.

### 2.2 What the comparison really shows

There is no single “best-practice URL pattern” shared by all winners. The repeatable system is more important:

```text
National/corporate brand authority
        ↓
Actual local operator or branch entity
        ↓
Services the branch really provides
        ↓
Geography the branch can really serve
        ↓
Proof: reviews, jobs, team, availability, guarantees
        ↓
One measurable call / quote / booking path
```

Scaled companies commonly use one root domain and folders, but they vary enormously in geo-page volume. Mr. Rooter and Orkin are aggressive; Molly Maid and Mosquito Joe are comparatively restrained. Therefore:

- “More city pages” is not the causal lesson.
- The common element is a coherent local entity tied to services, coverage, proof and conversion.
- National brands can rank imperfect or templated pages because they possess authority, brand demand, reviews and links a new site lacks.
- Large brands also carry duplicate patterns and architecture debt. We should not cargo-cult their mistakes.

### 2.3 The architecture decision for this franchise

Ranked options:

| Rank | Architecture | SEO/entity strength | Control | Risk | Decision |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Approved pages under the official FISH location path | Highest | Low/medium | Corporate dependency | Best if FISH will support it |
| 2 | One franchisor-approved local domain, explicitly linked from the official FISH page | Medium initially; can grow | High | New-domain cold start and trademark approval | Best practical controlled option |
| 3 | One approved neutral local brand/domain | Medium/low initially | High | Must explain affiliation; weaker FISH brand continuity | Fallback if branded domain is denied |
| 4 | Corporate-approved subdomain | No inherent ranking advantage over folders | Medium/high | Still depends on corporate DNS/approval | Only for organizational reasons |
| 5 | Town subdomains or many exact-match domains | Fragmented | High apparent, low real | Thin authority, maintenance, doorway/entity confusion | Reject |

Google says subdomains and subdirectories are a business choice, not a ranking shortcut. Google also has systems intended to prevent exact-match domains from receiving too much credit and policies against doorway/scaled low-value pages. Sources: [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide), [ranking systems guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide), [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies).

### 2.4 Recommended information architecture

```text
/
├── window-cleaning/
│   ├── residential/
│   └── commercial/
├── gutter-cleaning/
├── [approved-adjacent-service]/
├── care-plans/
├── pricing-and-estimates/
├── location/
│   └── bridgeview-il/
├── service-areas/
│   ├── hinsdale-il/
│   ├── burr-ridge-il/
│   ├── oak-brook-il/
│   ├── downers-grove-il/
│   └── [southern-priority-market]/
├── projects/
│   └── [real-job-slug]/
├── reviews/
├── about/
└── estimate/
```

Core rules:

- Every indexable page has one real purpose and self-canonicalizes.
- Service pages answer service intent; town pages answer local-provider intent; project pages prove the intersection.
- Town pages link to relevant services; service pages link to towns only where there is proof/demand.
- A project links to its service and town, creating a fact-based internal graph.
- Ad-only variants that differ mainly by headline should be `noindex` or consolidated rather than added to the SEO index.
- Do not generate a service × town page merely because the combination exists in a spreadsheet.
- Structured data must describe visible, truthful business information; it does not create local relevance by itself. [Google structured-data policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/sd-policies).

### 2.5 Page publication gate

Publish a town or service×town page only if at least four of these are true:

1. Search/query evidence exists.
2. Historical jobs or leads exist.
3. The town fits route and margin economics.
4. We have original photos or a real project.
5. We have a town-specific review/customer story.
6. Property stock creates a genuinely distinct issue.
7. The offer, scheduling, or service mix differs.
8. Internal pages can link to it naturally.

This turns programmatic SEO into **programmatic evidence**, not programmatic prose.

For the first five town hubs on a cold domain, use an even stricter target before indexation:

- two completed jobs or documented service examples;
- one attributable local testimonial;
- three original local images;
- accurate route/response information;
- a useful local price/quote-factor section;
- at least one genuinely local FAQ;
- links to the real office entity, relevant services, neighboring towns and projects.

If we need a town-specific paid destination before that evidence exists, create a useful conversion page but keep it out of the organic index until it passes the gate.

## 3. What sophisticated home-service advertisers actually optimize

### 3.1 The marketing system, not the media channel

The mature loop is:

```text
Demand source
  → tracked call/form/message
  → answered and qualified
  → estimate or appointment
  → booked job
  → completed/paid job
  → contribution margin and crew hours
  → repeat / membership / referral
  → outcome returned to ad platform
```

The biggest difference between a sophisticated operator and a weak local advertiser is often not ad copy. It is whether the platform is trained on a button click, a raw lead, or a completed profitable job.

Google explicitly supports offline imports and Enhanced Conversions for Leads, and says primary versus secondary conversion configuration affects Smart Bidding. [Google offline conversion documentation](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7012522).

### 3.2 Evidence from scaled operators and large datasets

| Evidence | Finding | Proper interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| [Comfort Keepers + Invoca](https://www.invoca.com/customers/comfort-keepers) — reported franchise case | Across 650+ territories, keyword/session call tracking and conversation outcomes were returned to ad systems; vendor reports 59% more annual paid-search call volume, 50% lower cost per call and 50% higher phone sales conversion | Strong evidence for closing the call-attribution gap across locations; results are vendor-reported, not an audited universal benchmark |
| [F.H. Furr + LSA/ServiceTitan](https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/google-local-services) — reported operator case | Reported 225 additional booking leads, 80% conversion and $200,000 attributed revenue during the cited period | Direct booking and real-time capacity can matter; old case and emergency-trade economics limit transferability |
| [CallRail 1.1M-lead analysis](https://www.callrail.com/blog/callrail-releases-benchmark-report) — reported aggregate benchmark | Home services missed 14% of calls; Google Ads, organic and GBP were major sources of qualified leads and appointments | Phone operations are part of marketing ROI; vendor dataset composition may not match this franchise |
| [Invoca 60M-call analysis](https://www.invoca.com/uk/reports/the-invoca-call-conversion-benchmarks-report-home-services-2025) — reported aggregate benchmark | Only 55% of callers reached a person; 37% of digital calls were leads and 46% of those converted on the call | Large leakage can occur after media acquisition; use as directional benchmark, then measure locally |
| [ServiceTitan 2025 residential report](https://www.servicetitan.com/press/residential-industry-report-2025) — reported operator survey | 47% of $10M+ contractors said unsold-estimate follow-up produced 11–15% of income | Estimate recovery is a revenue channel, not administrative cleanup |
| [LocaliQ 2025 benchmark](https://localiq.com/blog/home-services-search-advertising-benchmarks/) — reported 3,200+ campaign benchmark | Reported $66.69 average platform CPL for window cleaning and $90.92 overall home services | Platform lead cost is a directional planning input, not an allowable booked-job CAC |

### 3.3 Critical 2026 platform change

Google is migrating Local Services Ads into a specialized **Performance Max campaign with pay-per-lead goals**. It remains pay per valid lead, remains on Search and Maps, remains keywordless, and uses GBP/business-category data. The first U.S. phase began in August 2026; broader service-area businesses follow later. Historical performance reports will not transfer, and manual/vertical-level bidding changes. [Official Google migration notice](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17213585).

This means the account plan must distinguish:

- **Pay-per-lead PMax:** the successor to LSA, Search/Maps only, category/area driven.
- **Conventional PMax:** cross-network automation across Search, YouTube, Display, Discover, Gmail and Maps.
- **Search campaigns:** keyword/query-controlled intent capture.

Calling all three “Google Ads” obscures important economics and controls.

## 4. Recommended account architecture

### 4.1 Local Services / pay-per-lead PMax

Purpose: capture high-intent local demand through the existing legitimate business identity.

- Preserve one real GBP and advertiser identity.
- Do not create town profiles or virtual offices.
- Export historical LSA reports before migration.
- Reconcile GBP name, phone, hours, categories and location.
- Use real job and crew photos.
- Monitor/dispute invalid leads.
- Answer calls and messages quickly; responsiveness influences LSA performance.
- Separate materially different business categories if the migrated platform requires different targets/budgets.

Window cleaning is an eligible U.S. LSA category. [Google eligibility](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841/getting-started-with-local-services-ads-united-states).

### 4.2 Search campaigns

Do not create one campaign per town by default. Create campaigns around **intent and economics**, with geography applied through profitable service clusters.

1. **Brand** — capped; report separately so it cannot inflate acquisition results.
2. **Residential window cleaning** — core planned demand.
3. **Commercial/storefront** — separate buyer, funnel, repeat value and sales process.
4. **Gutter/adjacent service** — separate when seasonality, value or capacity differs.
5. **Controlled expansion** — broad match or conventional PMax only after downstream conversion data is reliable.

Initial residential ad groups:

- window cleaning near me
- residential window cleaning
- interior/exterior window cleaning
- window washing + priority town
- two-story/high-window variants if they reflect real profitable work

Initial negatives include jobs/careers, DIY/how-to, equipment/supplies, auto/windshield, replacement/repair if not offered, irrelevant housekeeping/janitorial, and out-of-territory locations.

Use **Presence** targeting rather than Google's broader default that can include people merely interested in the area. [Google geo guidance](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9376662).

### 4.3 Conventional PMax

Do not begin here. Test only after:

- qualified/booked conversion imports work reliably;
- enough meaningful outcomes exist for automation;
- brand traffic can be excluded from acquisition reporting;
- URLs and expansion are controlled;
- creative assets are real and approved;
- Search and pay-per-lead performance provide a baseline.

The failure mode is easy platform-reported conversions that are cheap but economically poor.

### 4.4 The conversion hierarchy

Capture every stage but bid toward the deepest stable stage with enough volume:

1. Raw inquiry
2. Serviceable/qualified lead
3. Estimate scheduled
4. Job booked
5. Job completed and paid
6. Care plan enrolled
7. Repeat job completed

Do not make a phone-button click, page view and booked job equal primary conversions. Eventually pass a conservative contribution value, not just revenue.

Suggested value concept:

`expected contribution value = first-job contribution + conservative repeat contribution − travel/route burden`

## 5. Website and landing-page strategy are related but not identical

The SEO site is a durable information and proof graph. A paid landing page is a controlled conversion experiment.

### SEO pages

- Broad enough to satisfy a durable intent.
- Linked and indexable.
- Built around authentic entity/service/location evidence.
- Updated with projects and proof.

### Paid landing pages

- Exact message match to campaign/service/offer.
- Minimal navigation when appropriate.
- Call and quote action visible immediately.
- Fast mobile load.
- Dynamic number insertion and click identifiers preserved.
- Can be `noindex` when variants are near-duplicates.

This resolves a false choice in the original conversation. We do **not** need 15 domains or 150 indexable pages to make ads locally relevant. A single approved domain can serve controlled paid variants while the organic index stays disciplined.

## 6. Social, direct mail and local activation

### 6.1 Meta's role

Meta is not a substitute for Search. It activates latent/event-driven demand and retargets proof.

For a modest territory, begin with:

- one broad service-area prospecting ad set;
- six to ten materially different creatives;
- a Higher Intent instant-form versus website/photo-quote test;
- one combined retargeting/recovery audience when volume is sufficient;
- booked/completed outcomes returned through CRM/CAPI.

Do not split into tiny town ad sets before there is enough data.

Creative systems to test:

1. Real before/after transformation.
2. Trust inside the home: uniforms, shoe covers, careful furniture/property handling, insurance—only when true.
3. Rain/touch-up guarantee as objection removal.
4. Event triggers: graduation, listing a home, party, holidays, spring pollen.
5. Truthful route-day availability.
6. Recurring convenience.
7. Commercial reliability and off-hour scheduling.

Meta reports that its conversion-lead optimization with CRM/Conversions API feedback improved cost per quality lead and lead-to-quality-lead rate in its own study; treat this as first-party directional evidence, not an independent guarantee. [Meta lead-generation guide](https://about.fb.com/ltam/wp-content/uploads/sites/14/2023/11/LeadGenerationGuide.pdf).

### 6.2 Nextdoor

Nextdoor is attractive because of neighborhood identity and homeowner concentration. Use it as a proof/recommendation network first, then run paid tests in two dense ZIP clusters. [Nextdoor home-services materials](https://business.nextdoor.com/en-us/local/local-experts).

Measure completed-job CAC and route efficiency—not messages or clicks.

### 6.3 Direct mail / EDDM

The sophisticated play is not blanketing Chicagoland. It is coordinating mail with operations:

- carrier routes around existing high-value customers;
- neighborhoods adjacent to already-booked jobs;
- property stock that fits the service;
- three drops rather than a one-off;
- unique number/URL/QR and source code;
- matched-route or holdout design where possible;
- arrival timed to real crew availability.

USPS EDDM supports carrier-route selection and demographic filters. [USPS EDDM](https://eddm.usps.com/eddm/select-routes.htm).

### 6.4 One job should create a neighborhood acquisition loop

```text
Completed job
  ├── review request
  ├── Nextdoor recommendation request
  ├── before/after creative
  ├── project page
  ├── GBP update
  ├── adjacent-route mail/door outreach where permitted
  ├── route-day Meta creative
  └── recurring plan / referral follow-up
```

That is how content, media and field operations compound instead of operating as separate chores.

## 7. The local conversion offer stack

The best home-service brands do not rely only on “quality service.” They reduce perceived risk and friction with concrete promises.

Potential FISH stack, subject to franchise approval and operational validation:

- Fast quote-response standard.
- Photo-assisted quote option.
- Clear explanation of what is included: glass, screens, tracks/sills, interior/exterior.
- Arrival-window communication.
- Property-care protocol.
- Rain/touch-up policy.
- Background-checked/uniformed/insured claims only if verifiable.
- Spring/fall or quarterly care plan.
- Residential bundles where margins support them.
- Commercial recurring schedules and minimal-disruption promise.

The landing page should answer four questions immediately:

1. Do you serve my property and ZIP?
2. Can I trust you inside/around my home?
3. What exactly am I getting?
4. How quickly and easily can I get a real estimate?

## 8. Non-obvious category insights

### 8.1 Page count is a lagging artifact, not the strategy

Mr. Rooter and Orkin have enormous local footprints; Molly Maid and Mosquito Joe are far lighter. The scalable advantage is an entity-proof-conversion system, not raw URLs.

### 8.2 National brands get away with pages a new domain cannot

Brand searches, links, reviews, age and branch networks can carry templated pages. A cold domain needs a much higher evidence density per page.

### 8.3 The franchise's operational data is the real programmatic SEO dataset

Jobs, property types, service mix, photos, timing, route clusters, recurring status and customer language can generate unique local pages. A language model inventing 1,000 words about Hinsdale cannot.

### 8.4 The phone desk can erase the best media campaign

Large call datasets show meaningful missed-call and non-conversion leakage. Media management must include answer rate, qualified-call rate, estimate booking and follow-up.

### 8.5 Route density changes allowable CAC

The same $300 job has different value 8 minutes away versus 50 minutes away. Town bidding should be based on contribution and crew time, not household income alone.

### 8.6 Reactivation competes with paid acquisition

Past customers and unsold estimates already know the company. Mature contractors treat these as revenue channels. Before raising Meta spend, compare the marginal return from reactivation.

### 8.7 Commercial and residential should not share one generic funnel

Commercial buyers want insurance, scheduling reliability, safety, references and recurring terms. Residential buyers prioritize trust in the home, care, clarity and convenience.

### 8.8 Social creative should be produced by operations

A separate “content calendar” will become generic. The job-completion workflow should trigger proof capture automatically.

### 8.9 A new local website can be useful immediately for ads even while SEO matures

Organic authority takes time. Paid landing-page control creates value sooner through message match, testing and attribution—provided the domain/brand is authorized.

### 8.10 The true moat is the feedback loop

Competitors can copy a page design, headline or coupon. They cannot quickly copy a dataset connecting source → query → call → booked job → margin → crew hours → repeat value.

## 9. What to build first

### Phase 0 — authority and baseline

- Obtain written franchise approval for domain, brand use, tracking, ad destinations and lead ownership.
- Export LSA history before the 2026–27 migration removes old reporting.
- Audit existing Google Ads, LSA, GBP, call tracking and agency access.
- Import 12–24 months of leads, jobs, ZIPs, revenue, crew time and repeat history.
- Define qualification and loss reasons.

### Phase 1 — measurement and intake

- One CRM/lead ledger.
- Dynamic call tracking where permitted.
- GCLID and consented contact capture.
- Qualified/booked/completed conversion imports.
- Immediate missed-call text and callback ownership.
- Weekly answer/booking QA.

### Phase 2 — controlled acquisition

- Existing legitimate LSA/pay-per-lead identity.
- Brand Search separated from acquisition.
- Residential Search.
- Commercial Search only with a distinct funnel.
- Gutter/adjacent campaign if approved and profitable.
- Presence-only geo targeting and route-cluster reporting.

### Phase 3 — controlled website

- One domain with 12–16 strong core pages.
- Five priority town hubs selected from actual economics.
- Paid variants as `noindex` where appropriate.
- Project-page workflow fed by completed jobs.

### Phase 4 — demand creation and compounding

- Meta creative/offer tests.
- Nextdoor recommendations and two-ZIP test.
- Route-timed EDDM experiment.
- Unsold-estimate recovery.
- Past-customer seasonal reactivation.
- Care-plan and referral system.

Scale a channel only when it produces incremental, serviceable, completed work within contribution and capacity thresholds. A cheap lead, high ROAS based only on revenue, or a full calendar can all conceal bad economics.

## Bottom line

The strongest services companies do not win because they found the perfect city-page URL. They combine:

1. One coherent brand/entity architecture.
2. High-intent Google capture.
3. A conversion experience matched to service economics.
4. Phone and estimate operations that prevent leakage.
5. Closed-loop bidding using qualified/booked/completed outcomes.
6. Local proof generated from actual jobs.
7. Retention, reactivation and recurring plans.
8. Territory and capacity controls that turn marketing into profitable routes.

For this FISH franchise, the correct adaptation is a **single approved controlled site, a disciplined core page set, project-driven local proof, LSA/Search first, visual and neighborhood activation second, and a measurement system tied to contribution and crew time.**
