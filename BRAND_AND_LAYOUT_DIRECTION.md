# Brand and Layout Direction

## FISH Window Cleaning — Dave Weishar, Chicago Western & Southern Suburbs

This brief converts the existing FISH identity into a locally persuasive website system and assigns a proven independent-operator reference to every page we intend to build. It follows the Unfairly market and landing-page disciplines: brand claims come from evidence, each page has one job, and the structure moves from promise to proof to action.

## Executive decision

Build a **proof-led local operator** site, not a prettier franchise brochure and not a luxury-home lifestyle brand.

The visual idea is **Local Crew. Clean Precision.** FISH red uniforms, trucks, logo, and recognizable field presence provide continuity. Dave, his team, real Hinsdale-area properties, and specific operating proof make the brand local. Crisp typography, restrained color, generous white space, and clear process modules make it feel current.

There is no single website worth cloning end to end. The best system is a disciplined composite:

- **Homepage visual and proof model:** [TRUST RPC](https://www.trustrpc.com/), with the tighter conversion economy of [TNG Window Washing](https://tngwindowwashing.com/).
- **Hinsdale/local hub:** [My Window Washing — Hinsdale](https://www.mywindowwashing.com/hinsdale), strengthened with the property and route specificity of [Sparkle Service — Hinsdale](https://sparkleservice.com/locations/hinsdale/).
- **Residential service page:** [Pane Perfection IL](https://paneperfectionil.com/) for sequence and home-care reassurance, plus [My Window Washing — Window Washing](https://www.mywindowwashing.com/window-washing) for scope and price education.
- **Commercial page:** [Above N’ Beyond](https://longisland-officecleaning.com/services/window-cleaning/) for scope discipline and walkthrough logic, plus [Supreme Clean](https://supremecleanwindowservices.com/commercial-cleaning/) for recurring-service positioning.
- **Project/case study:** [King of Kings — Bow project](https://www.kingofkingswindowcleaning.com/case-studies/window-cleaning-bow-wa/) for problem–method–result structure.
- **Estimate experience:** [Mt. Baker Window Cleaning](https://mtbakerwindows.com/) for low-friction qualification and explicit next steps.

Copy mechanics, not decoration. We should never reproduce another operator’s copy, exact visual styling, claims, or proprietary photography.

## 1. Brand truth we can build from

### What the existing FISH identity actually communicates

The official location page and its source establish a compact, recognizable system:

- Primary logo and uniform color: **FISH red**.
- White wordmark and high-contrast red field.
- Neutral charcoal copy on white and light-gray surfaces.
- Red trucks, red shirts, uniforms, shoe covers, clean-up practices, and background-checked crews.
- A local owner, real team, service-area list, and community involvement.
- An existing green estimate button that functions as an action cue, even though green is not part of the logo.
- Current website typography: Open Sans.

The page also contains valuable first-party visual material: Dave’s portrait, team-and-vehicle photographs, residential and commercial job images, gutter and pressure-washing examples, and community-service photography. Before reuse, confirm image quality and permission; do not upscale small web thumbnails.

### What to preserve

- The official FISH logo, with clear space and no recoloring, distortion, shadow, or lockup invention.
- Red shirts, trucks, and branded field equipment as the most credible recurring visual motif.
- The plainspoken service-business personality: dependable, careful, established, locally accountable.
- The connection to the official FISH location page and its authoritative phone/estimate process.
- Verifiable operating practices such as uniforms, shoe covers, floor protection, clean-up, recurring schedules, and background checks.

### What to leave behind

- Corporate mega-navigation.
- A video-first hero in which the visitor must infer the local value proposition.
- Long centered text blocks and generic franchise history before local proof.
- Multiple equally weighted actions such as careers, bill pay, franchise information, and estimate requests.
- Unstructured photo galleries without captions, locations, or a clear customer problem.
- Generic claims like “quality service” when a specific arrival, protection, walkthrough, or correction policy can be shown instead.

## 2. Visual system

### Color roles

| Token | Value | Role |
|---|---:|---|
| FISH red | `#E51837` | Logo field, brand accents, section markers, primary branded CTA where appropriate |
| Deep ink | `#141617` | Headlines, dark proof bands, premium contrast without drifting into generic navy |
| Charcoal | `#4D4D4D` | Body copy and secondary information |
| Paper | `#FFFFFF` | Primary canvas and text on red/dark surfaces |
| Warm mist | `#F4F3F1` | Alternating sections, cards, quote/process surfaces |
| Soft rule | `#E4E1E1` | Borders and dividers |
| Handoff green | `#34833F` | Optional, narrowly reserved for the official “Request estimate on FISH” handoff |

White on FISH red has a 4.65:1 contrast ratio; white on handoff green has a 4.70:1 ratio. Both meet WCAG AA for normal text. Do not use red for small body copy on gray, or green as decorative branding. If the official handoff button remains green, its unique color should signal “continue to the official estimate flow,” not compete throughout the page.

### Typography

- **Headlines:** a sturdy, slightly condensed sans such as Barlow Condensed or Archivo Narrow, used with restraint. It echoes the direct, workmanlike energy of the wordmark without imitating it.
- **Body and UI:** Source Sans 3 or Open Sans. Open Sans preserves continuity with the current site; Source Sans 3 is the cleaner modernization.
- Use sentence case for most headings. Reserve uppercase for compact labels, proof chips, and service metadata.
- Use tabular numerals for phone numbers, ratings, price examples, and job facts.
- Avoid script fonts, faux-luxury serifs, all-caps paragraphs, and ultra-light type over photography.

### Photography

The image hierarchy should be:

1. Dave/team on a real local job.
2. A recognizable local property context without exposing a private address.
3. Branded red shirts, truck, tools, and careful work in progress.
4. Before/after pairs showing a real visual problem.
5. Finished glass, gutters, storefronts, or exterior surfaces.

Every published job photo should carry four facts in the CMS: town, service, property type, and the problem or result shown. Avoid anonymous stock technicians, implausibly perfect AI houses, heavy teal/orange grading, or generic “sun through a window” imagery as primary proof.

### Graphic language

- Strong rectangular crops and occasional rounded cards; do not make every surface a floating pill.
- Thin red rules, small red location/service labels, and dark proof bands.
- Before/after comparisons, job-fact tables, checklists, and simple line icons only where they improve scanning.
- Motion should be limited to useful before/after controls, subtle reveal, and clear hover/focus states. No parallax, autoplay carousel, or decorative glass-shine animation.

### Voice

Specific, calm, neighborly, and operational. The strongest line is often the one that reduces risk:

- “Written price before we schedule.”
- “Shoe covers and floor protection for interior work.”
- “We text before arrival.”
- “Final walkthrough before we leave.”
- “Request a commercial walkthrough.”

Only publish a claim when Dave confirms the team can deliver it consistently.

## 3. Primary reference: what to mimic and what not to

### TRUST RPC — best overall visual reference

[TRUST RPC](https://www.trustrpc.com/) is the best look-and-feel benchmark because it is an independent Chicago-suburbs operator, uses real field photography, puts the phone and quote action in the header, and makes operating proof visible immediately. Its live page currently leads with a local-tenure label, a clear trust promise, quote/call actions, and a proof row for years in business, homes cleaned, reviews, and insurance.

Borrow:

- Full-bleed real-work hero with a controlled dark overlay.
- One local promise, two actions: quote and phone.
- Proof in or directly below the hero.
- A strong switch from dark hero to light editorial content.
- Service cards that lead to deeper pages.
- Process, reviews, seasonal offer, and FAQ in a coherent narrative.

Do not borrow:

- Its black-and-gold identity; FISH remains red, white, ink, and warm neutral.
- Any figures, offers, badges, or claims.
- The oversized luxury-editorial tone if it makes FISH feel precious or expensive.
- Its exact service taxonomy.

### Why Pane Perfection is not the master visual

[Pane Perfection IL](https://paneperfectionil.com/) is excellent for process, scope, pricing cues, and home-care reassurance. Its pale blue, generic iconography, and logo-dominant hero are less ownable than real FISH photography. Use its information sequence, not its aesthetic.

### Why Sun Window Cleaning is not the master visual

[Sun Window Cleaning](https://sunwindowcleaning.com/) is the cleanest modern composition of the group: dark split hero, concise copy, photo card, quote/phone pair, and proof chips. It is useful for spacing and hierarchy, but its navy/orange system is visually generic and its sparse homepage does not yet show enough local proof for Dave’s advantage.

## 4. Exact page layouts

### A. Homepage / local acquisition hub

**Primary job:** establish local trust, let the visitor self-select residential or commercial, and drive a measured estimate or phone handoff.

1. Utility strip: “Locally owned by Dave Weishar • Serving Chicago’s western and southern suburbs” plus phone.
2. Compact header: FISH logo, Services, Areas, Projects, About Dave; one estimate CTA.
3. Hero: real Dave/team/job image; local outcome headline; one-sentence proof; `Request a free estimate` and `Call (630) 757-3474`.
4. Proof bar: verified rating/count, insured/background-checked only if current, actual local customer/job figure, response expectation.
5. Residential/commercial path chooser.
6. “How it works” in four steps: request, written scope/price, protected clean, final walkthrough.
7. Three attributed local reviews linked to their source.
8. Recent work: three cards with town, property type, challenge, service, result.
9. Core services: windows first; gutters, pressure washing, fixtures/awnings as secondary offers only when approved.
10. “How we protect your home” photo-backed protocol.
11. Dave/team block with a real portrait and a short accountability statement.
12. Price drivers or real example ranges after invoice analysis.
13. Priority service cluster and route expectations.
14. Five to seven useful FAQs.
15. Short final estimate CTA and official-site relationship disclosure.

Reference mix: TRUST RPC visual system + TNG’s concise owner/process story + Giant Window Cleaning’s real-job proof density.

### B. Hinsdale local hub

**Primary job:** rank and convert broad local intent while proving genuine Hinsdale operating knowledge.

1. Town/service hero with a real Hinsdale job image and town preselected in the CTA.
2. Town proof above the first major scroll: verified jobs/customers, town-specific review, recognizable commercial proof if permissioned.
3. Services popular in Hinsdale, linking to canonical service pages.
4. “What changes the work here”: divided-light or historic windows, interiors, estate scale, mature trees, stone sensitivity, storefront timing—only facts the team verifies.
5. Two to four local project cards.
6. Quote factors and honest guidance.
7. Local process, arrival, weather, and route-day details.
8. Three to five local FAQs based on actual calls.
9. Only genuinely adjacent, territory-correct towns.
10. Final CTA with Hinsdale and source context carried into analytics/CRM.

Publication gate: three original local photos or one complete project; three real local operating insights; one town-specific quote or scheduling detail. Without those, do not publish the page.

Reference mix: My Window Washing’s section architecture + Sparkle’s property and route specificity. Remove their bloat, placeholders, and generic municipal prose.

### C. Residential window cleaning

**Primary job:** make scope, care, price drivers, and the household experience legible.

1. Outcome-led hero with quote and call.
2. Local review/credential strip.
3. Choose scope: exterior only; inside and out; optional detail add-ons.
4. Included-versus-add-on matrix covering glass, screens, tracks, sills, frames, storms, skylights, and hard-water treatment.
5. Home-care protocol: arrival text, shoe covers, drop cloths, furniture/pet care, final walkthrough—only confirmed practices.
6. Four-step service experience.
7. Real job examples with town, scope, and defensible price/range only after Dave’s invoice audit.
8. Price drivers and a simple “how we count windows” visual.
9. Attributed residential reviews.
10. FAQs: rain, access, presence, pets, screens/tracks, high glass, rescheduling, guarantee.
11. Estimate CTA with `residential windows` preselected.

Reference mix: Pane Perfection’s sequencing + My Window Washing’s buyer education + Renova’s in-home care details. Cut generic benefits and duplicated guarantees.

### D. Commercial window cleaning

**Primary job:** sell reliable recurring service and make procurement easy.

1. Hero: “Reliable window cleaning for Hinsdale-area businesses” with `Request a commercial walkthrough`.
2. Permissioned proof: Dave’s reported 12+ Hinsdale commercial customers only after validation; one named/attributed buyer quote; actual storefront photography.
3. Choose property type: storefront/restaurant, professional or medical office, community/nonprofit, condo/HOA/low-rise, multi-location local business.
4. Included scope and explicit height/access/service exclusions.
5. Suggested cadence: weekly/biweekly/monthly/quarterly as starting guidance, not a universal prescription.
6. Operating system: walkthrough/photo quote, written scope, COI/W-9/vendor setup if true, scheduling around traffic, access protocol, completion record, recurring invoicing.
7. Three commercial mini-case studies.
8. Buyer-specific FAQs.
9. Purpose-built bid form: company/role, address, property type, floors, scope, desired cadence, operating hours, locations, deadline or board-packet date.

Reference mix: Above N’ Beyond’s scope boundaries + Supreme Clean’s recurring-service expectation + Omega’s address/photo quote + Crystal Clean A1’s HOA buyer insight.

Do not reuse the homeowner form. Commercial buyers should feel that Dave understands vendor setup, access, frequency, reporting, and invoicing.

### E. Project / case study

**Primary job:** turn completed work into reusable proof for search, ads, sales, and social.

1. Result/problem + service + town headline.
2. Project facts: month/year, town, property type, service, scope, main challenge, method, crew/time if useful.
3. Why the customer called and relevant constraints.
4. Assessment and quote decision.
5. Work performed and property-protection steps.
6. Before/after pairs with descriptive captions.
7. Result without invented metrics.
8. What a similar local buyer should know.
9. Customer quote only with permission/source.
10. Links to the parent service, town hub, and estimate action.

Aim for 500–900 useful words. Do not create a page for an ordinary job unless the property, problem, method, or result is distinct.

### F. Estimate / official FISH handoff

**Primary job:** capture just enough context, attribute the lead, and make the next step obvious.

Step one:

- Name, mobile, email, service address/ZIP.
- Residential or commercial.
- Service selection.
- Preferred contact method.
- Optional notes/photos.

Step two adapts by buyer. Residential asks scope, approximate panes or photos, stories, screens/tracks/storms/skylights, and timing. Commercial asks property type, floors/access, cadence, hours, location count, walkthrough preference, and vendor requirements.

Confirmation must repeat what was submitted, state the real response expectation, explain whether the next step is a photo/satellite or on-site quote, offer click-to-call, and link clearly to the official FISH booking/location page. Carry source, campaign, town, service, and landing-page values into analytics and the operating record.

## 5. Sitewide design and content rules

- One page, one dominant buyer action.
- Proof before explanation; explanation before the final ask.
- Town pages explain geography; service pages explain the service. Do not launch a town × service URL matrix.
- A project page proves a real completed job; it is not a disguised keyword clone.
- No fake review widgets, unsourced rating counts, invented customer logos, or stock imagery labeled local.
- Do not use private residential addresses, faces, license plates, security details, or customer names without permission.
- Never publish “project coming soon.” Fewer complete pages beat visible placeholders.
- Make all valuable public pages server-rendered, linked in HTML, included in the sitemap, and eligible for indexing. Keep paid duplicates and confirmation pages noindex.
- The mobile experience gets a persistent call/estimate action, but not a screen-obscuring sticky bar.
- The official relationship should be explicit: locally owned FISH franchise; official phone and estimate destination; link to the authoritative location page.

## 6. What makes this feel like a $1M/year agency build

The non-obvious advantage is not a fancy hero. It is an evidence system:

- Every job produces an optional photo set, town/service metadata, a short field note, permission status, and a review request.
- Every published claim has an owner and a source.
- Residential and commercial buyers receive different proof and different quote questions.
- Each page preserves its referring town/service/campaign through the official FISH handoff.
- New town pages unlock only when there is proof, demand, and an operational reason to publish them.
- Commercial “paperwork” becomes a product benefit: COI/W-9 readiness, written scope, resident notice, access protocol, completion record, and board-packet deadline—where Dave can genuinely support them.

This is the brand: recognizable FISH field presence, local accountability, and a visibly lower-risk buying experience.

## 7. Immediate production inputs

Before design moves into high-fidelity comps, collect:

1. Highest-resolution approved FISH logo file, preferably vector.
2. Dave portrait plus two team photos.
3. Truck/uniform/equipment photo.
4. Six to ten residential job sets, including two in Hinsdale.
5. Three commercial job sets with customer-name permission status.
6. Current Google rating/count and six attributable reviews.
7. Confirmed service inclusions, exclusions, insurance/background-check language, arrival/rain/guarantee policies, response expectation, and commercial paperwork capabilities.
8. Twelve months of completed-job data sufficient to validate real price examples and route patterns.

Once those inputs exist, the next design step is a three-direction visual board using the same brand truth: **field-forward**, **clean editorial**, and **neighborly utility**. The recommended starting direction is field-forward with the information discipline described above.
